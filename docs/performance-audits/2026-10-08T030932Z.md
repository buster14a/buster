## Host-state observations and limits

All eleven lab summaries observed `benchpress`, AMD Ryzen 7 9700X 8-Core Processor, Linux x86-64 kernel `6.18.50-2-lts`, perf `7.2.9-1`, microcode `0xb404038`, amd-pstate-epp, the performance governor/EPP, boost enabled, and SMT active. The harness pinned measurement and throughput work to CPU 2. The `throughput/governor.txt` file is explicitly read from CPU0 by the trusted throughput runner and says `powersave`; throughput metadata records CPU2 but has no `host_qualification` object. Therefore that CPU0 snapshot says nothing about CPU2 governor state and does not show a state transition. The optional qualification header would record the selected CPU, but it was not emitted in this route. Load averages were approximately 1.0. The archived CPU information is point-in-time only and does not provide per-pair frequency records. The raw files do not establish IRQ relocation or SMT-sibling isolation; do not claim those controls. Benchmarking guidance recommends same-root rebuild, cross-root and A/A controls for dedicated-host calibration, but this frozen pull-comparison route contains none. #3025, #3031 and #3051 warnings identify identical measured instructions but differing wall time and explicitly recommend an A/A; #3026 warns that bootstrap and sign-test decisions disagree at the effect boundary without recommending A/A; no extra same-root or A/A control is inferred or claimed. The comparison config sets `require_identical_output=false`, although ten lab summaries report cross-variant output equality and #3027 reports different outputs; all eleven throughput corpora have equal paired output hashes and sizes. The profile produces usable evidence for the exact binaries and workload, but not those omitted calibration controls.

The comparison runs were sequential on one shared dedicated host, not simultaneous measurements across all candidates. Do not pool the eleven outcomes or interpret small candidate-to-candidate differences as a controlled head-to-head ranking. The 0.5% practical floor applies independently to each compiler-compare-v1 wall CI. A `below-floor` result means the CI excludes 1.0 but reaches inside the 0.5% practical floor, so the full CI does not establish faster or slower performance beyond that floor and does not establish the absence of a code effect. A CI including 1.0 is “no detectable difference,” not equality; its bounds can still admit larger changes. Instructions/cycles/phase/corpus outcomes explain observations but do not override the wall verdict. No measured candidate presently establishes a whole-compiler speedup.
