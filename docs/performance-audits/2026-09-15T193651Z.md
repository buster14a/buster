## The problem, with the population it affects

`ir_prepare_canonical_module` (`src/buster/lib/compiler/ir/ir_fast.c`)
validates at up to four boundaries: uncertified input, promotion output, FAST
input, and FAST output. For a producer-certified module in a build with
`BUSTER_IR_TRANSFORM_CHECKS` on, local promotion's mutation triggered a
promotion-output scan, and then the FAST input guard -- `if (input_certified)`
-- scanned the same, unmutated rows again before any transform ran. FAST's
output scan followed. Three scans per module; the middle one asked exactly the
predicate the first had just answered.

`BUSTER_IR_TRANSFORM_CHECKS` is `!BUSTER_OPTIMIZE || BUSTER_INCLUDE_TESTS ||
BUSTER_SANITIZE || BUSTER_VERIFY_IR_TRANSFORMS`. The CMake default builds
`ide` with `-DBUSTER_INCLUDE_TESTS=1`, so the affected population is the
trusted Clang-built Release `ide` that `test_self_host`, `bench_throughput`
and every local benchmark run, plus Debug, sanitizer and explicit
verification builds. Self-hosted stages are built `-DBUSTER_INCLUDE_TESTS=0`
and already ran one scan (the guard) after promotion; they are unchanged, as
is every uncertified-caller path, where the guard never ran.

A `gdb` breakpoint census on the baseline `-g0` compile of the unity unit
counted exactly three `ir_validate_canonical_module` calls, all from
`ir_prepare_canonical_module`. The baseline `perf record` profile attributed
10.2 % of self samples to the validator, the largest single symbol at `-g0`.

## The change

One local `bool validated` in `ir_prepare_canonical_module`: true while the
strict validator has passed over the module in exactly its current state. A
successful input or promotion-output scan sets it; promotion's mutation clears
it; the FAST input guard is now `if (!validated)`. Nothing else moves: the
uncertified input scan, the promotion-output scan under transform checks, the
FAST output scan, `-fverify-codegen`, and CFG publication keep their trust
preconditions. The guard still scans a certified module promotion left
untouched (nothing has proven those rows) and in optimized non-test builds
(no promotion-output scan ran). Producer certificates remain input
certificates only.

Regressions (`ir_fast_test.c`, `ir_promotion_test.c`): a certified module
promotion changes and a certified module promotion leaves alone are prepared
with `IR_FAST_ALL`; under `BUSTER_BENCH_ALLOCATIONS` the boundary counters
must read promotion-output `1`/FAST-input `0` versus `0`/`1`, with FAST-output
`1` and a passing independent revalidation in both; the corrupted-module
skip case must still spend exactly one FAST-input scan; and a failed
promotion-output scan must leave `fast.functions == 0` and `!fast_complete`.

## What this does not establish, and what it leaves

- No wall-clock speedup is admitted from this host; the instruction count is
  the claim. A dedicated-host paired timing series would settle the cycle/wall
  share.
- The remaining two scans per module (promotion output and FAST output) are
  the transform-check contract for test/Debug/sanitizer builds and were not
  touched. Whether the FAST output scan could be made incremental over the
  rows FAST actually rewrote is a separate question with different trust
  preconditions.
- `preparation_calls=2` and `preparation_publication_functions=9582` (twice
  the 4,791 lowered functions) show preparation and CFG publication being
  entered twice per module (driver, then codegen). Publication's second entry
  appears cheap (`cfg_builds=4791`) but its exact cost was not measured here.
