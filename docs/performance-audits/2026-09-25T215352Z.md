## Decision

Every x86-64 `ide cc` invocation spends about **123.8 M instructions** before
it looks at its input: `buster_x86_metadata_prewarm` (29.1 M) decodes the
generated x86 tables and `machine_x86_64_exact_prewarm` (94.7 M) derives exact
plans, dense encoding tables, fixed byte templates and the closed
expansion-shape cache from them. The work depends only on the compiler binary.
It is **98.4%** of a one-function compile, 89% of `hello.c`, 80% of
`tests/basic_c_operations.c`, and 25-75% of Buster's own library files compiled
one per invocation; the same one-function file compiled for AArch64 costs
1.8 M in total. Most of it prepares entries that the compile never reads.

The first removal is prototyped here: the shape cache now **registers** its
336 closed-set queries (hashes plus parameters) and resolves each shape on its
first serial lookup, instead of running 336 generic selector searches up front.
`machine_x86_64_exact_prewarm_all_shapes` keeps the complete resolution, with
the duplicate-signature agreement check, for every caller that starts a gang.
On every measured small workload it removes **29.6-37.3 M instructions per
invocation** (6-30% of the total) with byte-identical output. On the held-out
family (18 library files, not measured before the prototype was frozen) the
total falls **14.0%**. On the stage-1 self-compile it removes 27.6 M (0.11%).
Peak RSS is unchanged. These are instruction counts from an observation build,
not timings; the matched 9700X experiment below is required before any speed
claim.

## Governing invariant

For each closed-set signature, the published token is the resolution of the
**first registered query** with that signature, exactly as the eager walk
published it. Resolution is a pure function of that query and the immutable
generated metadata. The plan-table slot a token names depends on preparation
order. Tokens are opaque and never reach output bytes, relocations, `-v`
statistics, bootstrap traces or diagnostics. A worker never sees a pending
entry: every `lane_run` that may emit x86-64 is preceded by
`machine_x86_64_exact_prewarm_all_shapes` (`compiler_parallel_prewarm`, the
test harness), and every fill site states `BUSTER_CHECK_SERIAL_INITIALIZATION`,
so a caller that forgets fails loudly instead of racing.

Failure behaviour narrows but stays closed. Registration failures (unknown
mnemonic, capacity, guard collision) still disable the whole bridge. A failed
per-shape resolution disables the bridge from that point, and the affected
function takes the existing reject path (canonical fallback, or
`codegen.machine-fallback` under `-fno-machine-fallback`). A shape outside the
closed set still misses. The eager tripwire that invalidated all 336 shapes
together moves to the complete-resolution audit, which tests run.

## Tradeoffs, reported separately

- **Cold start and small translation units**: 30-37 M fewer instructions per
  x86-64 invocation, measured on every small family above. A multi-input
  invocation pays the prewarm once per process and gains the same absolute
  amount once. The opt-in `-fcompile-jobs` path still resolves every shape
  before its gang, so it gains nothing.
- **Large input**: -0.11% on stage-1; a large compile reaches more shapes and
  resolves them anyway.
- **Memory**: peak RSS is unchanged within run-to-run spread on every family
  ([`peak_rss.txt`](evidence/2026-09-25T215352Z/peak_rss.txt); tiny
  27.2-27.7 MB and stage-1 1,130 MB on both). The registration table adds
  159,744 bytes of `.bss`, of which 139,776 bytes are written per x86-64
  invocation; `.text` grows 11.7 KB.
- **Self-host**: no generated source or static table was added; the compiler's
  own source grows by about 200 lines and the self-host fixed point by 16,560
  bytes.

## Remaining input-independent floor and ranked frontier

After this change a one-function x86-64 compile still retires 88.5 M, about
86.7 M of it table preparation. The used-versus-prepared census
([`scout_ledgers.md`](evidence/2026-09-25T215352Z/scout_ledgers.md), section A)
shows what the rest prepares and what a small compile reads:

| Rank | Work (per x86-64 invocation unless stated) | Ir | Read by a small compile | Owner |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Dense GPR encoding tables | ~25 M | 3-47 of 64 tables | #124 lab branches (`codex/124-*`); not touched here |
| 2 | NUL-distance index over the 1.73 MB pool | 16.4 M | 476-2,053 of 1,726,254 entries | unowned; single reader `buster_x86_metadata_string_offset_terminated` |
| 3 | Variable-memory encoding tables | ~14 M | 0-362 of 6,912 entries | unowned; separate from the #124 preparer |
| 4 | Forms/operands base64 decode, coverage decode | ~9 M | 184-990 of 11,013 forms; coverage never | unowned |
| 5 | Remaining shape and template preparation | ~11 M | 6-62 queries, 7-195 template rows | follow-up to this change |
| L | Self-host only: compiling the generated headers | 5,164 M (20.8% of stage-1) | n/a | #1028 (initializer validation walks) |
| L | Self-host only: SSA finish walks for once-initialized locals | 75 M measured prototype, ~2% bound | n/a | unowned; not part of this change |

An oracle replay that prepares only what each workload itself reads bounds the
total deletable preparation at 71% of the base one-function compile (this
change realizes 29.6 points of it), 56% of `hello` with a link (24.1), 29% of
`basic_c_operations.c` (21.6) and 15% of `string.c` (11.1), byte-identically.
That bound uses each workload's own read set and is not a design; a real design
needs the same serial first-use or pre-lane discipline as this change.

## Limits

Callgrind counts instructions retired by an x86-64-v3 build under emulated
CPUID (`haswell` codegen target on the observation runs). It does not count
kernel page-zeroing, cache or TLB effects, or the AVX-512/VBMI decode kernels
that the trusted build uses. No wall-clock, PMU or 9700X measurement was made,
and none is implied by the percentages above.
