`2026-08-22e` (Linux x86_64, Zen 4 7940HS; **give the x86 selector's prepasses
a row layout and a candidate list instead of five more walks of the
instruction list**).  The 2026-08-21 stage-1 branch-miss survey put
`machine_select_canonical_function_x86_64` at 11.2% of all stage-1 branch
misses (~2.82 M of 25.17 M) against 6.28% of cycles — the compiler's largest
single mispredictor; measured again on the tree this is based on, after
`2026-08-21d` and `2026-08-22a` had landed, it is 12.0%.  Before selecting
anything it ran five complete traversals of every function's instruction
linked list: the use/def ordinal walk, two alias sweeps that each gathered a
block's rows into an array before walking them back, the aggregate slot walk,
and the branch-fusion walk.  The
survey's three largest single lines — the alias sweep's loop-exit `jne` and
the `LOAD`/`STORE` opcode tests inside it — were 2.4% of the run's branch
misses on their own.

Measuring the population first settled the design.  A self-compile selects
3,814 functions, 112,966 blocks and 1,215,633 rows, and **every block's rows
are already the dense id range starting at its `first_instruction`**:
1,102,667 of a possible 1,102,667 links are `id + 1`.  Block *array* order is
not id order, so only 1,002 functions are one flat ascending run end to end,
but within a block the linked list carries no information the id range does
not.  The four opcodes the alias sweeps and the fusion pass act on —
`LOAD`, `STORE`, `DEREFERENCE`, `BRANCH_IF` — are 374,623 rows, 30.8% of the
population; the other 69% were visited by three passes purely to fall
through.

The prepass region is now one walk of the linked rows that records, besides
the value facts it already accumulated, each block's row count and the
offsets of its candidate rows, plus whether any block's rows were not its
dense range.  The two alias sweeps and the branch-fusion pass read that
compact candidate list; the classification pass and the main selection walk
count through the dense ranges.  The per-block gather array the sweeps used
is deleted outright.  Anything that leaves a block's rows out of that shape —
the selection reordering test relinks two of them on purpose, and its
stream-equality assertion is what covers this path — makes the walk write the
gathered program order into `MachineSelectionRowLayout.rows`, and every pass
reads that instead; the shared type and its accessor live in
`machine_select.h` so the AArch64 selector, which has the same
four-traversal shape, can adopt them.

Two smaller finds from the same profile came with it.  `IrFunction` now
carries an `opcode_summary` word that `ir_function_add_instruction` maintains
for a named list of rare opcodes, published as known by
`ir_module_add_function` so that hand-built IR — the bitcode fixtures write
rows straight into `instructions` — reads as unknown and keeps its scan.
`codegen_canonical_x64_function_shape`, the sibling find `2026-08-19c` left
untaken, was rescanning every row of every function for atomics and inline
assembly; six of the 3,814 functions hold one, so the summary answers for the
rest and a 78 MB stream over the row array disappears.  The Windows
dynamic-stack scan asks the same way.  And the pass that gives each aliased
load its local's register now reads the 128,091 values the sweeps actually
aliased rather than asking all 1,006,179 whether they have an alias — that
one line was the single largest branch-miss site left in the function.

Fourteen alternating same-source, CPU-pinned, non-multiplexed pairs against
`5588bece` (both compilers building the pre-change tree, outputs
byte-identical) favor the candidate 14/14 on instructions and reduce the
median from 10,247,122,622 to 10,229,590,734, **-17,531,888 (-0.1711%)**.
Branches fall 1,898,763,830 -> 1,887,313,834 (**-0.6030%**, 14/14), branch
misses fall 25,184,647 -> 24,713,844 (**-1.8694%**, 12/14), and L1d misses
fall 125,589,374 -> 124,067,203 (**-1.2120%**, 12/14).  L1d loads rise
3,706,678,782 -> 3,714,452,786 (**+0.2097%**): the row counts and the
candidate offsets are new reads, and they are what removed a third of a
pass's row visits.  Cycles are noise at this size — the median moves +0.3661%
and favors the candidate 4/14 — so, as in `2026-08-19c` and `2026-08-20a`,
the honest carrier is the retired-instruction count.

Two things about that miss number are worth writing down.  It is smaller than
the 2.4% the survey's three lines represented, because those three lines were
the gather loop and its two opcode tests while the walk that survives carries
most of what is left: matched records at 9,999 Hz put the function at about
11.4% of the run's misses before and 10.0% after, roughly an eighth of its
own.  And it is sensitive to how quiet the machine is — an earlier fourteen
pairs taken against `644bfaa5` during another agent's matrix run put the same
binaries at -0.6839%, with an A-side spread three times this run's.  Take
one to two percent as the range, and the direction as settled at 12/14.

One variant was measured and reverted.  Writing every row's offset into the
candidate list unconditionally and advancing the cursor by the predicate —
the compaction shape the SIMD rules ask for — removed 1.2 M branches and
0.4 M misses but cost **+7.3 M instructions**, unchanged when the array was
`restrict`-qualified, so the unconditional store is not an aliasing artifact.
At four pairs each that trade did not pay, and the branchy append stayed.

The final `test_self_host` reaches a byte-identical 35,391,680-byte fixed
point: stage 1 retires 10,234,040,244 instructions against the pre-change
tree's 10,246,935,288 (**-12,895,044 / -0.1259%**) and stage 2 retires
75,147,829,793 against 75,216,522,433 (**-0.0913%**).  Those two stages
compile the changed tree, which is why they show less than the same-source
pairs above: the new arrays and their bookkeeping are 90 more lines for the
compiler to read.  Release `test_all` passes 309,205 assertions in all 39
modules and the full local CI combination matrix
(`test_all_combinations_ci`) is green; remote a64/Windows coverage stays
pending on CI, and this change wants it — the AArch64 selector is untouched
but `machine_select.h` and the IR row are shared.

Three leads are left where this one stops.  The walk that survived is now the
function's largest mispredictor, and its cost is the per-row operand loop —
a variable-arity scatter into `value_use_counts`/`value_use_blocks` — not the
block loop; splitting operands into a stream the pass can run over without
re-entering each 64-byte row is the next honest unit.  `branch_fusions` is a
16-byte record per value, zeroed for all 1,006,179 values in a compile and
read only at the 51,692 `BRANCH_IF` rows: keying it by block, or writing its
zero at the branch row itself, removes about 16 MB of stores per compile.
And the AArch64 selector still makes four traversals of the same list.
