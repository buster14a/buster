## What changed and what stays required

1. Sparse extras' remapped-order check joins the existing ID-bounds loop.
   Ordered keys are remapped without payload sorting or sort scratch. Disordered
   keys keep the original pack/merge/unpack path. The first candidate wrote all
   mapped keys before sorting; its dense disordered fixture cost about 0.6%
   extra instructions. Fusing the check removed that adverse effect. All those
   earlier observations remain retained.
2. Value definitions and extra keys consume the old-to-new map before the row
   permutation. A cycle then carries its row and canonical source forward and
   consumes map entries to identity. No inverse array is allocated for either
   identity or moved functions. Scratch requests fall from 8N to 4N bytes for
   instruction maps; this is allocation traffic, not an RSS claim.
3. A nonascending slice kind gets a bounded, allocation-free proof using two
   address partitions. Each partition must be monotone and nonoverlapping; no
   slice may cross the split. Descending slices and two reversed spans can stay
   in place. Overlap or more fragmented layouts retain the copy fallback. Every
   publication re-proves current slices; direct edits after reopening cannot
   inherit a stale certificate.

The general precise address-sort prototype was rejected: complete-compiler
instruction counts rose 0.26-0.38%, exceeding the work screen. A more expensive
proof is therefore not used to eliminate every conservative copy. The remaining
copies in the table below are deliberate fallback work, not a claim that all
nonaliasing layouts are recognized.

Ownership, invalid-ID, null-slice/count, overflow, exact-tail and termination
checks remain. Dense block-ID spans, sources, values, labels and relocation
ownership remain unchanged. The deferred validator/publication walk fusion in
#1406/#1424 and its measurement hold are untouched.
