## Fit

- **Serial minimum.** The smallest multi-input population measured, two equal
  TUs at 11.7 ms serial, gains about 1.2× with a lower bound above 1.0, and
  every `count` row gains. No measured population is protected by staying
  serial, so `COMPILER_DRIVER_LANE_MIN_WORK_BYTES` is 0.
- **Per-byte and per-input cost.** A least-squares line through the `size`
  series gives about 3.24 ms per 48.1 KB TU and an intercept of about 5.6 ms
  (process start, link and publication), that is 0.067 µs per byte. `tiny`
  then leaves (10.7 − 5.6) / 112 ≈ 0.045 ms per TU, of which its 162 bytes
  explain 0.011 ms. The remaining 0.035 ms per input is about 513 bytes at the
  same rate: `COMPILER_DRIVER_LANE_INPUT_WORK_BYTES` = 513.
- **Per-worker floor.** With that charge `tiny` is about 75.6 KB of estimated
  work. It loses at 10.8 KB per worker (7 workers, 0.933×) and still gains at
  18.9 KB per worker (4 workers, 1.105×) and 37.8 KB (2 workers, 1.148×). Any
  floor between those two points caps `tiny` at 4 workers, a configuration the
  receipt measured. `COMPILER_DRIVER_LANE_WORK_PER_WORKER_BYTES` = 16 KiB, never
  capping below two workers. Every `equal`, `count`, `size` and `skewed` row
  keeps at least one 48 KB TU per worker and is unaffected; `tiny` at 7
  workers and at 13 SMT workers runs 4.

## Limits

- The fit uses seven serial medians and three `tiny` points; the floor sits
  between two measured points rather than at a measured crossover.
- File bytes exclude headers, so header-heavy inputs are underestimated. That
  can cap or keep serial a population that would gain, never one that loses.
- The SMT and 8-core `machine` series are not refit; the cap applies to them
  through the same estimate.
- Any change to the lane entry cost moves all three values.

The validation run must show `tiny` at 7 workers at or above 1.0× with
`lane_gate=capped` and 4 observed workers, and every other row unchanged.
