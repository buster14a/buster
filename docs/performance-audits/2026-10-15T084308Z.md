## Bounded candidate and complete work

The trial defers multi-chunk instructions only when edge-copy sources exist
and neither inline assembly nor stack-memory certification needs contiguous
pre-split rows. It builds a temporary chunk directory and lets the existing
stable splitter read spans directly into the final layout. Single chunks retain
the ordinary borrowing path. Canonical remapping, block/edge publication,
split order, duplicate-destination selection and metadata remapping remain.

The 420 splitting functions avoid 924,399 intermediate instruction-row copies
(22,185,576 payload bytes), while adding 1,605 directory entries (12,840 bytes)
and span/index work. The census bounds all potentially eligible directories,
including no-split functions, at 2,035 entries/16,280 bytes; exact eligibility
within that upper bound also depends on whether copy sources exist. No-split
multi-chunk functions still need their ordinary final materialization and may
pay the new directory cost. The complete compiler observations below include
that cost and the span-based loop changes; the copy reduction alone is not a
net instruction result.

Six alternating paired `instructions:u` observations, one warmup per arm,
are complete and nonmultiplexed. All runs use immutable binaries, the same
absolute input/output paths and native ELF compile/link flags.

## Disposition and reproduction

Reject the measured bounded candidate and retain the baseline implementation.
The avoided bytes do not justify the added machinery under the declared
whole-compiler rule. This does not prove every possible preselection/layout
algorithm neutral; a broader design needs a distinct concrete experiment and
new complete accounting. #4244's bounded research disposition is measured-neutral,
not a delivered compiler optimization or closure of #296.
