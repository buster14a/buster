`2026-09-25T223847Z` (Linux x86_64 Firecracker VM, Xeon @ 2.80GHz (4 vCPU);
instrumentation only; **IR construction row streams: dense module-wide rows
remove 24% of THP zero-fill and 6.7% of 4 KiB first-touch faults on the unity
self-compile, no timing claim**).

## Decision

Keep a **draft prototype**, not a performance acceptance. The canonical IR
construction arrays that `c_lower_to_ir_with_options` sizes from body tokens
(`IrFunction.instructions`, `instruction_canonical_sources`, `values`,
`blocks`) are now carved as four module-wide **row streams** from the same
summed capacities; each function is placed at the stream cursors and, when it
finishes (success or rejection), the cursors advance by the rows it kept
(`c_ir_row_streams_trim`). Capacities become counts, so any later append takes
`ir.c`'s ordinary growth path out of the stream. The instruction stream is
64-byte aligned and its rows are 64 bytes, so no row straddles a cache line.

On the frozen unity self-compile the object is byte-identical, and the
per-process page work drops deterministically:

| Stage-1 `-g -c` (per-process counters) | main `ade6ac4b` | row streams | Δ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Minor faults, 4 KiB pages (THP `madvise`) | 282,760 – 282,978 | 263,838 – 263,971 | **−19.0 K (−6.7%)** |
| Peak RSS, 4 KiB pages | 1,123.5 – 1,123.9 MB | 1,047.8 – 1,048.5 MB | −75.5 MB (−6.7%) |
| 2 MiB zero-fill faults, THP `always` (`thp_fault_alloc`, idle host) | 975, 975, 976 | 740, 740, 741 | **−235 ≈ −470 MiB cleared (−24.1%)** |
| Peak RSS, THP `always` | 2,006.1 – 2,006.7 MB | 1,526.4 – 1,527.4 MB | −479 MB (−23.9%) |
| `-fno-frontend-ssa` minor faults (4 KiB) | 274,444 | 265,940 | −8.5 K (−3.1%) |
| Instructions, `object.c` under Callgrind (x86-64 without AVX-512) | 1,046,004,886 | 1,045,987,048 | −0.002% |

No wall-time, cycle or speedup is claimed. Deterministic fault and zero-fill
counts are the evidence; timing belongs to the admitted dedicated-host route
(see *Runnable dedicated-host experiment*). The largest credible upside, by
arithmetic on the #906 audit (`2026-09-23T144607Z`), is ~1.2–1.5% of stage-1
wall on a THP=always host and a few tenths of a percent at 4 KiB pages.

## Why this representation (ranking and re-ranking)

Three scouts, one per responsibility (access patterns, lifetime/ownership,
alternative design with counterarguments), plus an integrator-owned census,
ranked candidates by cost, reach, evidence and cost to falsify:

1. **Frontend expression facts re-derived from token ranges** — the largest
   compute cost (Callgrind: `c_parse_validate_lowering_constraints` 28.8% of
   Ir; the #906 9700X capture: 24.10% of user cycles). Already owned by #1028
   with several active branches, so no competing prototype was started; the
   whole-TU query census below is contributed there instead.
2. **Kernel first-touch zero-fill**, cross-cutting: the #906 capture puts
   77.7% of kernel cycles in `clear_page_erms`, and system time is
   0.090–0.113 s of 1.545 s wall. Its 2.10 GB peak RSS is reproduced here only
   with THP `always` (2.006 GB; 1.124 GB at 4 KiB), so the dedicated host very
   likely zero-fills ~1.95 GB of 2 MiB pages per stage-1 compile for ~1.13 GB
   of touched 4 KiB pages. **~0.8 GB (42%) of that clearing covers bytes the
   compiler never writes.** Per-site residency (below) attributes it to
   per-unit worst-case-capacity arrays whose used prefixes are interleaved with
   untouched tails. Unowned. Chosen.
3. **`IrInstruction` row width** (64 B, ~29 sweeps, 87% of arrays only 8-byte
   aligned). Real, but #791's measured slices cut DRAM fills up to 4.4% with no
   cycle or wall change, so a row-width redesign is not the best-supported
   lead. Alignment is obtained for free by the chosen change.

Within (2), the IR construction arrays are the largest single producer and the
most-traversed representation, so they are the first slice. The preprocessor's
per-file arrays are the second (see *Remaining producers*).

## Old and new representation

| | main | row streams |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allocation | per function, four arrays at `body_tokens×3+params×4+16` rows (blocks `body_tokens+8`), placed back to back in the TU arena between that function's other allocations | four module-wide streams reserved from the sum of the same capacities; each function placed at the cursors |
| After lowering | capacity tails stay between functions; capacity > count | cursors advance by kept rows; capacity = count |
| Layout seen by consumers | one array per function, arbitrary 8-byte alignment, tails and other allocations between functions | same pointers and counts; rows of consecutive functions contiguous; instruction rows 64-byte aligned |
| Handed-out bytes (TU cursor), stage-1 | 380.0 + 71.3 + 95.0 + 126.3 MB | identical totals, reserved once at lowering start |
| Kept rows, stage-1 | 1,524,516 instr (97.6 MB), 1,524,516 sources (18.3 MB), 1,315,524 values (21.0 MB), 168,748 blocks (10.8 MB) | identical |
| API / ownership | `IrFunction` fields in the caller's arena | unchanged fields, still the caller's arena |

The canonical IR stays independent of frontend IDs; no new arena, owner,
lifetime, callback or dependency is introduced.

## Access ledger (consumers of these rows)

From the access scout's probe and field-touch matrix
([scout_access.md](evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/scout_access.md)):

| Consumer (stage-1) | Rows swept | Scope |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| lowering append + `c_ir_ssa_finish` liveness and remap | 1.52 M × 3 | per function |
| `ir_promote_function` (3 scans on 3,932 functions) | 1.45 M × 3 | module loop |
| `ir_validate_canonical_module` × 2 (ownership, values, blocks) | 1.52 M + 1.34 M, ×~2.8 each | **module-wide** |
| FAST fold/address/dce + `ir_rewrite_compact` | 1.52 M × 6 | module loop |
| `ir_function_publish_cfg` (chain, pools, in-place permutation of 1.13 M rows, `next` clear) | 1.34 M × 4 | module loop |
| x86-64 selector (use walk, alias sweeps, classification, fusion, selection) | 1.34 M × ~4 | per function |

About 29 sweeps, 40.8 M row visits. Every sweep reads through
`function->instructions`, so every one of them now walks dense, line-aligned
rows. The simulated-cache run on `object.c` (D1 48 KiB, LL 2 MiB, the regime
where the IR exceeds the LLC) shows that the *cache* effect of that density is
small: D1 read misses −0.9%, LL read misses −0.09%, LL write misses −0.66%,
instructions flat. The change's measurable benefit is kernel page work, not
CPU work, consistent with #791.

## Lifetime ledger (these rows)

From the lifetime scout
([scout_lifetime.md](evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/scout_lifetime.md)) and the
integrator's phase probe:

- Producer: `c_lower_to_ir_with_options`, one function at a time.
- Last consumer: codegen of that function; `object_from_canonical_codegen_module`
  reads only `IrFunction.symbol/source` and `debug_locals` afterwards. Residency
  until process exit is required by the whole-module codegen retry
  (`codegen_generate_canonical_module_with_trace`), unchanged here.
- Error path: a rejected function is trimmed in place like a lowered one, so
  its rows remain valid (no consumer reads them; the compile fails).
- Post-lowering growth: only `ir_promote_global` appends (values). With exact
  capacity the first append doubles and moves that value array out of the
  stream (`value_grows`), which is why the memory-form gain is smaller.
- On main the arrays occupied **200.4 MB of resident 4 KiB pages for 130.3 MB
  of live rows (+54%)**, plus 37.4 MB of rows written and then dropped before
  codegen; the stream layout lets the next function overwrite dropped rows.

The TU arena itself is otherwise unchanged. The ledger also records ~432 MB of
dead-but-resident frontend storage (semantic model, raw lex arrays, a pooled
lowering arena) and one ownership hazard (below); neither is changed here.

## Per-site residency (what the kernel actually zero-fills)

A disposable probe ([residency_probe.patch](evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/residency_probe.patch),
never merged) logs every arena allocation ≥64 KiB and reads
`/proc/self/pagemap` at each driver phase boundary.
[residency_4k.txt](evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/residency_4k.txt) and
[residency_thp_always.txt](evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/residency_thp_always.txt)
hold the complete reports (monotonic TU ranges; reused scratch regions are
reported separately because their pages are shared).

| Site (large functions only) | resident 4 KiB | resident THP `always` | handed out |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `c_gen.c` instructions | 84.2 MB | **281.0 MB** | 297.3 MB |
| `c_gen.c` blocks | 7.9 MB | **66.0 MB** | 68.8 MB |
| `c_gen.c` values | 18.4 MB | 47.3 MB | 46.4 MB |
| `c_gen.c` canonical sources | 8.8 MB | 31.5 MB | 30.8 MB |
| `c_source.c:c_lex_dispatch` per-file tokens | 44.4 MB | **137.3 MB** | 384.2 MB |
| `c_source.c:c_translate_source` checkpoints/offsets | small | **114.3 + 57.4 MB** | 384.2 + 126.2 MB |

Single large tables with one contiguous used prefix (e.g. `CParseResult.types`,
634 MB handed, 13 MB used; `debug_locations`, 1.31 GB handed, 10.5 MB used)
cost almost nothing at either page size: their untouched suffix is never
faulted. The waste comes from **many medium arrays, each a used prefix
followed by an untouched tail, interleaved**.

## What disappeared and what appeared

Removed (stage-1, deterministic):

- 19.0 K first-touch faults and 75.5 MB of resident 4 KiB pages.
- 235 2 MiB zero-fills (~470 MiB of kernel clearing) under THP `always`.
- 64-byte-row cache-line straddling in ~7/8 of instruction arrays.
- Rows written then dropped no longer strand pages: the next function
  reuses them.

Added:

- One pre-pass over declarations (13.5 K on stage-1) to sum capacities, four
  stream allocations, and a constant-time trim per function. No bytes are
  copied. Instruction count on `object.c` under Callgrind is flat (−0.002%).
- Reservation timing: the same total is handed out at lowering start rather
  than per function. On Windows, where `os_commit` is `VirtualAlloc(MEM_COMMIT)`,
  peak commit is unchanged but is charged earlier in the compile.

## Alternatives and counterarguments

From the design scout ([scout_design.md](evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/scout_design.md)),
then re-ranked by experiment:

- **Build in lowering scratch, copy the kept prefix out** (the first
  prototype, [rejected_copy_variant.patch](evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/rejected_copy_variant.patch)):
  −15.8 K faults and −450 MiB THP clearing, but 147.7 MB copied per stage-1;
  bodies of ~330 K–380 K tokens **aborted** where main succeeds (its scratch
  fit test ignored the rest of the lowering scratch, 411–825 B per body
  token); +9% faults for single 250 K–310 K-token functions. Rejected.
- **Tighter capacity estimate** (#52's scope): per-function kept rows per body
  token are p50 0.85, p90 1.41, p99 2.67, max 16.75 (instructions) and p99
  0.52 (blocks). A 1.5× estimate still regrows 183 functions and leaves ~47%
  tails interleaved — about half the THP benefit, with regrowth copies.
- **Row alignment only**: one line on main, gives the straddling fix without
  any page-work benefit.
- **THP disabled for the process**: a policy knob, not a representation fix;
  the cheapest falsifier of the THP argument (below).
- **Counterarguments to keep**: the benefit is kernel page work, not CPU work;
  the absolute wall upside is small; peak commit on Windows is unchanged;
  #531 would need per-lane streams; the memory form loses half the gain to
  promotion regrowth unless promotion reserves once.

## Remaining producers (not changed here)

- **Preprocessor per-file arrays**: raw lex tokens and shapes
  (`c_lex_dispatch`, capacity translated bytes + 17) and checkpoints/offsets
  (`c_translate_source`, capacity bytes + 2) are ~333 MB resident under THP
  `always` for ~50 MB touched. Their used prefixes are live only while the
  file's include frame is active (raw tokens) or for the source map
  (checkpoints), so the same stream-and-trim shape applies.
- Promotion regrowth: `ir_promote_global` could reserve its known additions
  once instead of doubling.

## Runnable dedicated-host experiment

Execute only through the admitted, exclusively leased 9700X route
(`docs/agents/benchmarking.md`, `tools/throughput/DEDICATED.md`); nothing
here was run there.

1. Record `/sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/{enabled,defrag}` and kernel
   version; the predicted effect depends on them.
2. Build main and the candidate serially in the **same configured root**,
   freezing each binary outside it, plus the required same-root rebuild and
   cross-root controls.
3. Page-work census (no timing, may also run on hosted executors):
   `python3 docs/performance-audits/evidence/2026-09-25T223847Z/measure_first_touch.py
   --baseline $BASE --candidate $CAND --pairs 4 --output census.json -- cc
   -Isrc -Ibuild/generated -DBUSTER_UNITY_BUILD=1 -DBUSTER_INCLUDE_TESTS=0 -g -c
   src/buster/apps/ide/ide.c`. Predeclared falsifier: with THP `always`,
   fewer than 100 fewer `thp_fault_alloc` per compile rejects the THP
   rationale; at 4 KiB, fewer than 10 K fewer minor faults rejects the
   density rationale.
4. Timing: `"$BENCH" run --baseline $BASE --candidate $CAND --baseline-id
   ade6ac4b --candidate-id $HEAD --cpu $CPU --machine-id $ENV_ID --lock-file
   $LOCK --output $RESULTS --profile ci --mode all --pairs 30 --warmups 2
   --self-host-root $FROZEN_ROOT --self-host-generated $FROZEN_GENERATED
   --require-identical-output`, after an A/A with one immutable binary.
   Predeclared expectation from arithmetic only: ≤1.5% stage-1 wall with THP
   `always`, ≤0.5% at 4 KiB; a result inside the A/A band is a no-change
   disposition, not a failure to retry.
5. Counterfactual: repeat 3–4 for main with THP disabled for the process
   (`prctl(PR_SET_THP_DISABLE)` wrapper). If main without THP matches the
   candidate with THP, the benefit is policy-shaped and the next producer
   (preprocessor arrays) is worth less than modelled.
