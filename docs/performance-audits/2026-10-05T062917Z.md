## Correction and disposition

This is an addendum to [2026-10-04T110828Z](2026-10-04T110828Z.md), published originally in #2619 at `f3afb6cced493141ad3f805c551d0c6c5cc5a826`. The original audit and its C model/results are preserved unchanged. Owning implementation question: [#2616](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/2616). No production change or new compiler timing/count result is claimed.

The earlier audit says that collecting branch expansions into batches needs at most three passes because tiers only grow. Tier monotonicity alone does **not** establish that whole-function iteration bound. An insertion can increase the distance of another branch that was in range in the previous batch. A later batch can activate that branch, then another. The number of tier changes of one fixup and the number of global fixed-point iterations are different quantities. A conservative termination argument can charge each changing iteration to at least one previously unperformed tier increase; it must use the finite total of admitted tier increases, rather than a universal three-iteration limit.

The archived model uses a restricted common-target family and verifies matching final sizes/hashes against its serial arm. Its 117–676x instruction reduction is an observation about that model family, not a proof of a general production relaxation pass bound. The original real-compiler trigger/control, source-supported superlinear mechanism and model results remain valid as reported; they establish no production speedup or general relaxation correctness. No new benchmark replay was needed to identify this logical overclaim.

A future batch implementation must retain monotone tier selection and validate a fixed point against newly out-of-range branches after insertions. Add cascading threshold-crossing controls, including branches initially in range, in addition to the original common-far-target family. Keep the production applicability and full encoder metadata/target correctness gates with #2616. No stronger three-pass promise should be made without a separately proved algorithm.
