`2026-10-04T105803Z` (Linux x86_64, GitHub-hosted ubuntu runner; **canonical
IR representation audit: current rows vs split hot/cold, compact indexed and

## Decision

**Keep one canonical IR representation for every phase: the current
`IrInstruction` row (64 bytes, three payload pointers), the parallel 16-byte
`IrValue`, the pre-publication `next` chains and the dense `IrPublishedCfg`.**
None of the three alternatives measured here (split hot/cold, compact
inline-operand record, chunked stable storage) pays for a wholesale
migration: their component gains are real but bounded, each loses on at
least one consumer class the production pipeline runs on every row, and the
migration surface is about 500 production and 270 test mentions of
`IrInstruction` across nine subsystems, including hand-built negative-oracle
fixtures that write rows directly.

One **bounded, local change is admitted to a predeclared matched-build
experiment**, not to production: move the construction-time `next` link out
of the 64-byte row into a per-function `u32` side array owned by `ir.c`
(construction, compaction, publication; cleared at publication exactly as
today). It changes no semantic, no id, no invariant of
[the construction protocol](../canonical-ir-construction.md), and the lab
measured the `next`-only ownership walk 3.2× faster on the stage-1 unit
(12.2 → 3.8 ns/row, three independent processes, both ISAs, both frontend
forms; 2.3× when the unit fits the cache). Its modeled whole-compile return
is small (about 30 ms of this VM's 1.77 s frontend+prepare time; less on the
dedicated host), which is why it goes to the harness rather than to `main`.

Everything else in this audit is a modeled or measured *negative* result
that should stop future reopenings unless the premise changes: see
[What loses where](#what-loses-where-pathological-workloads).

| **B** split hot/cold | any consumer that needs a cold field per row: the selectors' main loops (immediates, targets, symbol on ~48% of rows), validation's operation checks, the definition chase for immediates (1.13×); in-cache units, where its +37% instructions are not repaid (validator 1.11× slower on object.c); rows with ≥ 65,535 operands (large `ARRAY`/`AGGREGATE` initializers) need the escape on every operand access | `defs_cold`, `validate` in cache, callgrind Ir |
| **C** compact indexed | the same cold-field consumers, worse (extension indirection on 58.9% of rows: `defs_cold` 1.24×); compaction (1.12×: extension table copied and remapped); call- and initializer-heavy functions (3.7% of rows have more than two operands and pay pool + branch); every operand access site carries the inline/overflow branch | `defs_cold`, `compact`, `uses` in cache |
| **D** chunked stable | every sweep (directory indirection and 64-row boundaries: 1.08–1.20×), every random access by id (1.14×), tiny functions (median body 105 tokens ≈ 90 rows: two chunks, 28% padding; +12% bytes on stage-1); its stable addresses solve a problem the row streams already solved for the serial frontend | `census`, `uses`, `defs`, `chain`, `append` |
| **E** value folded into its defining row (modeled, not built) | block parameters need synthetic rows; 7 bytes of value facts (category, alignment, qualifiers) need a home in a row with one spare byte; `values[v]` indexing changes meaning for every consumer and for label metadata; the measured definition chase is latency-bound on near rows, so the removed indirection is worth little | `defs_hot` across layouts |
| Function-at-a-time reordering of the pipeline to make any layout's sweeps cache-resident | priced at an 8–9% ceiling with the FAST admission guard preserved | #792 audit `2026-09-19T010900Z` |

## Where a representation change would pay, and where it would not

- **Construction** (c_gen, 90 emit sites building a 64-byte `IrInstruction`
  by value and committing it): A with row streams is already allocation-free
  for rows; its remaining per-row cost is the operand slice allocation and
  the two later re-pools. B and C are 21–22% faster in the lab's append
  because they skip the per-row allocation, not because their rows are
  smaller. Inline operands in the existing 64-byte row (the first two ids
  in place of the `operands` pointer, 96.3% coverage) would capture the same
  effect without changing the row size, but every operand access site would
  gain a count branch (about 1,800 `operands` field accesses across the
  frontend, `ir` and the backends, of which codegen's 1,347 include
  machine-IR operands); the measured traversal gain of that idea (C's
  `uses`, `defs_hot`) is nil. Rejected as a standalone change.
- **Traversal**: narrow sweeps are where the row is wasteful (8% of each
  line used), and that is measured. But most production narrow sweeps run
  function-at-a-time right after the function's rows were written or
  compacted; the cold module-wide ones are promotion's discovery scan
  (whose removal #856 measured neutral) and the validator's ownership proof
  and publication's remap walk, both `next` walks — covered by the admitted
  side-array experiment.
- **Rewriting**: compaction is instruction-bound (~152 Ir/row) and
  layout-neutral; the FAST `removed`/`replacements` scheme is right for a
  bounded pipeline. No change.
- **Use-def maintenance**: counts rebuilt per pass cost 7.5 ms per rebuild
  on stage-1 and are rebuilt about three times (DCE, selector use walk,
  eBPF/debug consumers). A persistent use list costs more than that in
  storage and in unlink work at every retract/truncate/compaction. No change;
  this preserves the "no new permanent use-list representation" contract in
  [validation boundaries](../ir-validation-boundaries.md).
- **Serialization**: not a current consumer. The invariant to preserve is
  that, after publication, operands/targets/immediates of a function are
  contiguous pools (`ir_cfg_pool_operands` guarantees it), so a future
  serializer can translate pointers to offsets in one pass. No change.
- **Parallel processing**: ids are function-local, so per-function work
  needs no id rebasing in any layout; the serial points are the module-wide
  row-stream cursors (would become per-lane streams) and TU-wide
  type/symbol publication (#531). Chunked storage adds nothing here.

## Migration boundaries and invariants for the admitted experiment

Scope: `ir.c`, `ir_cfg.c`, `ir_promote.c`, `ir_fast.c`, `ir_append.h`,
`ir.h`, and the hand-built fixtures that write `next` directly. Outside
`ir.c` the row's `next` is read at exactly five production sites: three chain
walks in `c_gen.c` (the atomic-aggregate access check and the two
place-recovery repairs), `bootstrap_trace.c`'s pre-publication trace, and
`machine_select.c` through `ir_block_next_instruction`; the Wasm, eBPF, LLVM
and SPIR-V emitters never read it (they consume published spans). Tests
mention the row field 29 times across `ir_test.c`, `ir_cfg_test.c`,
`ir_construction_protocol_test.c`, `ir_fast_test.c`, `c_test.c` and
`bitcode_test.c`. The frontend's 90 emit sites and the selectors' row
consumers are untouched.

1. `IrFunction` gains `IrInstructionId* next` (one `u32` per row, same
   capacity rule as `instruction_canonical_sources`, carved from the row
   streams beside the sources). `IrInstruction.next` is removed; the freed
   four bytes stay reserved so the row remains exactly 64 bytes
   (`BUSTER_CT_CHECK(sizeof(IrInstruction) == 64)` stays).
2. `ir_block_link_committed`, `ir_block_insert_instruction_after`,
   `ir_block_retract_tail`, `ir_block_truncate_after`,
   `ir_block_next_instruction`, `ir_function_instruction_owners`,
   `ir_rewrite_compact`, `ir_cfg_publish_instruction_rows` and
   `ir_function_invalidate_cfg` read and write the array instead of the row
   field. Publication clears the array (one `memset`, or nothing: the span
   form never reads it) instead of the per-row `next` rewrite.
3. The block-row family of invariants is unchanged: a row belongs to exactly
   one chain, chains are acyclic, `last_instruction` is the tail, no row
   follows a terminator, results bind to their row. The validator remains
   the independent oracle and `ir_function_publish_cfg` the termination
   check every producer passes.
4. Hand-built IR in tests (the 29 mentions above) writes
   `function->next[id]` instead of `instructions[id].next`; negative oracle
   cases keep every invalid shape representable, including cycles, shared
   tails and a `last_instruction` the chain never reaches.
5. Acceptance is the existing matched-build contract: byte-identical stage-1
   objects, `test_self_host` fixed point, `test_all`, `test_mode_matrix`,
   then `tools/uarch_lab.py compare` on the dedicated host in two rounds with
   an A/A cross-root control. Predeclared stop: if neither round shows a
   wall-time improvement whose interval excludes zero, close the experiment
   as `status/measured-neutral` and keep the row as it is; the lab's
   component result stands on its own and must not be re-measured to find a
   better number.

A second, modeled-only candidate is recorded without admission: publish
spans without permuting rows (84.4% of rows and their 12-byte sources are
moved in place today, plus a 5.2 MB write-only `instruction_remap`), proving
span disjointness by sorting block starts instead of requiring block-id
order. It touches the validator's published-shape check and every consumer
that assumes spans ascend with block ids, and #857's publication-handoff
slice regressed in matched builds; it needs its own census before any code.

## Limits of this evidence

- Hosted VM, no PMU, nested paging, 4 vCPU shared host: absolute times are
  not the dedicated host's. Ratios were stable across three processes, two
  ISAs and two frontend forms, which is what the lab is for.
- Kernels model consumers; they are not the consumers. `validate` is the
  traversal third of the production validator; `compact` reproduces
  compaction's time to within 12%; `census` stands for several
  narrow scans whose exact field sets differ.
- Layouts B, C and D were built by copying a published module, so their
  construction-order variants inherit A's publication permutation; a native
  producer could order them differently. Their pools are per function, as
  A's are after publication.
- The object.c in-cache rows for `A_pub` `census`/`uses`/`defs` are
  cold-biased by the kernel loop (the production array is touched by fewer
  kernels than the lab copies) and are not cited above; use the `*_con`
  comparisons for that unit.
- Whole-compiler effects are modeled from the September field-touch
  inventory at `ade6ac4b`, re-read against the current source for the
  passes named; the pass set is unchanged by #1307 except that rows are now
  64-aligned.
