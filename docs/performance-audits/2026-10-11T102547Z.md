## Workload

Every arm compiles the same input, the base revision's unity self-host source,
from the base checkout:

```sh
ide cc -Isrc -I<base build>/generated -DBUSTER_UNITY_BUILD=1 -DBUSTER_INCLUDE_TESTS=0 \
  -target x86_64-unknown-linux -march=znver5 \
  {-g0 -c -o out.o | -g -c -o out.o | -g0 -fsyntax-only} src/buster/apps/ide/ide.c
```

The change is on the default path and needs no tree, so there are two arms,
both with default flags: A is the base, and D is the candidate. Callgrind ran
with `--cache-sim=no --branch-sim=no`.

## Why

The #3102 lowering census (comment of 2026-10-11 09:33, main `a6705764`) found
`c_ir_root_conditional` at about 145 M Ir inclusive. A throwaway counter build
saw these figures:
- 827,414 calls, 47,384 of them true;
- 772,167 calls (93.3%) scanning a range that holds no `?` token;
- 8.25 M tokens scanned in all.

The scan can answer true only after it finds a `?` inside its range. Its
paren peeling only narrows the range, and `c_ir_scan_delimiter_group` never
moves the index past `end`. A range with no `?` is therefore refused exactly.

## Change

- `c_ir_question_prefix_build` fills `question_prefix[o]`, the number of body
  tokens before offset `o` whose `punctuator` field is `C_PUNCTUATOR_QUESTION`.
  That is the scan's own test, without a kind check. The array lives in
  lowering scratch and is built the first time a query asks, once per body.
- `c_ir_range_may_hold_question` answers from two loads. A range outside the
  body, or a builder without a body, answers "may", which runs the scan.
- `c_ir_root_conditional` is now an inlined wrapper. The unchanged scan,
  renamed `c_ir_root_conditional_scan`, runs only when the range may hold a
  `?`. Reference-check builds run the scan behind every refusal and check
  that it agrees.

The first candidate left the wrapper out of line. Clang then inlined the scan
into it, and every call paid the scan's prologue and epilogue, about 15 M Ir
over the 830 K calls. Marking the wrapper `BUSTER_INLINE` keeps the scan out of
line and the refusal down to a few instructions at each call site. This one
change separates the two candidates' figures below.

The first candidate, with the out-of-line wrapper, measured −74.5 M (−0.31%) on

The predicate's own cost on `-c -g0`:
- **Base.** `c_ir_root_conditional` costs 154.6 M inclusive: 145.5 M charged
  to `c_gen.c` plus 9.1 M of inlined `c_internal.h` helpers.
- **Candidate, out of line.**
  - `c_ir_question_prefix_build` costs 16.4 M for 5,981 bodies and 2.25 M body
    tokens.
  - `c_ir_root_conditional_scan` costs 17.0 M for the ranges that hold a `?`.

- **Change.** The whole-compile saving of 96.5 M is 62% of the base
  predicate. The out-of-line rows alone, without the inlined range tests, fell
  by 78%.

`-fsyntax-only` runs no lowering, so its −0.002% is rebuild noise.

## The same pattern elsewhere: negative results

**`c_ir_has_root_comma`: not shipped.**

A throwaway candidate, kept as a diff, not committed, did three things:
- it filled a second prefix array of `,` tokens in the same build loop;
- it refused comma-free ranges the same way;
- it ran on top of the shipped change.

On `-c -g0` it measured:
- `c_ir_has_root_comma`'s scan fell from 26.9 M to 4.6 M inclusive;
- the shared build grew from 16.4 M to 26.9 M;
- the inlined range tests ate most of what was left.

The whole compile moved by −1.66 M (23,940,401,951 → 23,938,744,688), or
−0.007%. That is below the declared 0.05% bar and inside the 0.03% rebuild
noise.

**`c_ir_group_fact`: not attempted, by census.**

The scanning arm is the only part a count could help, because groups longer
than `C_IR_GROUP_FACT_SCAN_LIMIT` already read the classifying pass. That arm
is at most 18.8 M for all six facts together: 88.2 M inclusive, less the
69.4 M `c_ir_group_facts_build` called beneath it.

Only `ROOT_QUESTION` and `TOP_COMMA` reduce to the presence of a single token,
and their share of the arm is smaller still. The arm's scans are also bounded
at 32 tokens. The ceiling is below the 0.05% (≈ 12 M) bar, so no candidate was
built.
