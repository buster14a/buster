*The token position index is built in one pass.*
`c_parse_position_index_build` walked the preprocessed stream three times: once
to count the `vector_size` and `_Alignas` identifiers, once to record their
positions into arrays sized by that count, and once to match delimiters.  Every
pass branches on a token kind no predictor learns, and the LBR survey put the
three loops at 3,55% of the compile's mispredictions with the duplicated
identifier filter named as the fusable half.  The counting pass is gone; the two
position runs grow by doubling from empty, which is what lets the fill share the
delimiter pass.  Both populations are tens of tokens in a million, so the run
stays a few cache lines.  This was the largest single win of the session.

**What did not pay, and why it is the same failure twice.**

*The capacity-counting ladder in `c_analyze_semantics`, made branchless:*
**+156.926.699 instructions (+1,5508%), 0/11**, for -129.463 branch misses.
The loop classifies each of the 2,94 M preprocessed tokens with an if/else-if
ladder over `;`, `,`, `(`, `[`, `{` and the closers, and the LBR survey put it at
2,41% of all mispredictions.  Replacing the ladder with one table lookup per
token and a compare-and-add per counter produced a 45-instruction loop body
against the ladder's ~8: the ladder is cheap precisely *because* it exits early,
and a branchless form pays for every counter on every token.  Hoisting the loop
into its own function to stop the nine counters spilling inside a caller that is
one of the largest functions in the tree recovered a third of it and no more
(+105 M).  Cycles were flat either way — the added work is perfectly
ILP-friendly — which is exactly the reading `AGENTS.md` forbids treating as a
win.  **A mispredict is worth ~13 cycles; do not spend 35 instructions a token
to remove one that fires on a fraction of them.**

*The selector's separate scan for `IR_OPCODE_LOCAL` rows:*
**+16.510.698 instructions (+0,1634%), 0/11**, with no reproducible miss win.
`machine_x86_64.c:4267`-`:4268` is the top of the cache survey — 11,8% of the
compile's DRAM fills between two lines, the largest single item in it — and it
is a second stream over the 64-byte-per-row instruction array that the walk
below streams anyway.  `IrFunction` was given a list of its LOCAL rows, filled
by `ir_function_add_instruction` under the same `IR_OPCODE_SUMMARY_KNOWN`
marker the opcode summary uses, so the prepass reads four bytes per local
instead of streaming every row.  The first measurement said L1d -3,21%
(10/11); the second, after folding the appender's new test into the existing
`IR_OPCODE_SUMMARY_TRACKED` bit test, said **-0,16% (6/11)** — the win did not
reproduce, and DRAM fills went the wrong way in both.  This is
`2026-08-22T091548Z`'s lesson arriving from the other side: a miss share says
where a line's traffic goes, not how much would disappear if the line did, and
the rows the prepass fetches are *compulsory* — the walk immediately below pays
for them whichever pass touches them first.  **The selector's DRAM share is not
a lever; stop ranking it as one.**  A third attempt, fusing the classification
into the walk itself, is also recorded as failed: it needs the local's size
before a use can disqualify itself against it, and some locals are used before
their defining row is walked, so the fused form changes which locals promote
(the conservative version was not output-neutral; dropping the guard was
byte-identical here but silently drops disqualifications).
