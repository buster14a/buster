`2026-08-22f` (Linux x86_64, Zen 4 7940HS; **no lever in the intern probe loop
or the entity scope walk — both tables measured at their floor, and one nearby
branch measured on four trees and rejected**).  Hotspot #7 of the 2026-08-21
stage-1 branch-miss survey: `c_symbol_intern`'s open-addressing probe and
`c_parse_lookup_entity_symbol`'s bucket chain nested inside a scope chain.  The
survey put them at 2.78% and 1.29% of all 25.17 M stage-1 misses, the first
mispredicting at 1.9x its cycles share; on this tree — after `2026-08-21d` and
`2026-08-22a`-`e` took six of their siblings and about `-350 M` instructions
with them — they still hold **2.89% and 1.38%**, so nothing about their weight
was borrowed from the others.  Probe outcomes are unpredictable by
construction, so the only things that could pay were *fewer* or *shorter*
probes.  Both walks were instrumented and neither is short of either.  **No
source change**; this entry is the measurement, the ceilings, and one rejected
candidate that turned into a lesson about branch-miss deltas.

**Method.** Non-shipping counters on both walks — probes per intern, chain
length per bucket visit, scopes walked per lookup, name-length histogram, peak
load factors, plus a full bucket-occupancy sweep at the end of
`c_analyze_semantics` where `CParseResult` is still live — dumped through
`string_print` after `compiler_driver_execute_c_single` returns (both call
sites: the single-input path and the multi-input loop).  One instrumented
compile of the unity `ide.c` yields the whole distribution.  Figures below are
from `e34f7675`; the same measurement on `b2c52677`, `7c544f12`, `644bfaa5` and
`5588bece` agreed to within 0.1% on every one of them, so these are properties
of the workload, not of a tree.

**The intern table is at its floor.**  931.139 calls resolve 27.314 distinct
names — 903.825 hits, 27.314 inserts — and the probe histogram is

    1 probe   827.192  (88,84%)   cum  88,84%
    2 probes   65.166  ( 7,00%)   cum  95,84%
    3 probes   20.710  ( 2,22%)   cum  98,06%
    4 probes    7.673  ( 0,82%)   cum  98,88%
    5 probes    4.231  ( 0,45%)   cum  99,34%
                                  max 38

— a mean of **1,2065 probes per call against a floor of 1,0**.  Peak load
factor is `27.314 / 65.536 = 41,7%` after two doublings from the initial 16Ki,
exactly the "at most half full" policy the code documents.  The whole probe
surplus is 0,2065 probes/call, about 192 K extra probes across the compile; a
hypothetical perfect hash would remove roughly 1 M of 10,23 G instructions.
The `2026-08-08k` rules on this hash (byte FNV beat an 8-byte SWAR; the
one-byte prefix prefilter measured `+5,8 M`) were never in danger of being
retested, because the table's output is already at the theoretical minimum.

**The entity buckets are Poisson-ideal.**  48.716 entities in 65.536 buckets
(load 0,743), longest chain 6, and the occupancy histogram sits on the
random-hash expectation at every bucket size:

    occupancy  1: 23.582   (poisson 23.165)
    occupancy  2:  8.627   (poisson  8.610)
    occupancy  3:  2.026   (poisson  2.133)
    occupancy  4:    386   (poisson    396)
    occupancy  5:     48   (poisson     59)
    occupancy  6:      3   (poisson      7)

A better hash cannot exist for this key set.  546.377 lookups (377.668 hits,
168.709 misses) walk **2,484 scopes each** and touch **1,789 chain nodes** in
total; 43,5% of the 1.357.399 bucket visits land on an empty bucket and cost
one load.  The cost is therefore not chain length — it is that a lexically
scoped lookup asks the same question once per enclosing scope, over 37.919
scopes: 35,3% of lookups answer in one scope, 28,5% in two, 32,6% in three or
more, 3,6% start from an invalid scope.

**The memoization ceiling was measured too, and it does not clear the bar.**
An idealized 64Ki-entry direct-mapped cache of `(scope, symbol) -> entity`,
flushed wholesale on every `c_parse_scope_add_entity` because any add can
shadow a parent binding, hits **47,5%** of lookups (259.695 of 546.377); the
same-key-as-the-previous-lookup rate alone is 18,1%.  Half the lookups avoided
is real, but the cache costs 1 MB of table to shorten a walk whose whole
working set is a 256 KB bucket array, and it needs a generation stamp checked
on every lookup and bumped on each of 48.716 adds.  Caching the resolved entity
on the token has the same shape and the same shadowing-invalidation problem.
Not worth it at this function's weight — 1,38% of misses is about 0,1% of
stage-1 cycles at Zen 4's ~13-cycle redirect.

**One removable branch was found next door, and measuring it four times is the
real result of this audit.**  It is not a probe branch: `c_symbol_key` chooses
between "first and last eight bytes" and a byte loop on `name.length >= 8`, and
stage-1 name lengths split that test **53,7% / 46,3%** (500.331 names under
eight bytes, 269.462 in [8,16], 161.346 over 16) — an almost maximally
unpredictable data branch, executed once per intern.  Clang answers the short
arm with a masked gather, a `vpmovzxbq`, a `vpsllvq` and a four-step AVX-512 OR
reduction, twelve instructions whose other effect is a `vzeroupper` on all
three of the function's return paths.  The rewrite reads both words
unconditionally and masks: the tail offset `(length - 8) & long_name` collapses
to zero for a short name so `high` re-reads `low`'s word and the zero mask
discards it, and a page-crossing guard (`pointer & 4095 <= 4088`, taken for
~0,17% of pointers) keeps the unconditional load inside the caller's page, with
the byte loop retained behind it — folded from the last byte back, because the
indexed-shift form is re-vectorized into the very reduction being removed.  The
hot path becomes ten branchless instructions with no `zmm`, no `vzeroupper` and
no stack canary (`c_symbol_intern` 979 -> 1136 bytes); a standalone
differential test against an `mprotect`ed page confirms the two arms agree
bit-for-bit at every offset and length up to an unmapped boundary, 35 of them
through the guard.

The identical patch was measured on four successive trees as main moved under
it, fifteen same-source CPU-pinned pairs each, order alternated inside every
pair, outputs byte-identical, paired medians:

    7c544f12   +3.285.342 (+0,031%)  -488.509 (-0,025%)  -220.261 (-0,88%)  14/15
    644bfaa5   +3.289.047 (+0,031%)  -488.670 (-0,025%)  -207.290 (-0,83%)  14/15

**The instruction and branch deltas are reproducible to a fraction of a
percent of themselves — ±8 K on 3,29 M and ±500 on 488,7 K — across four
different baselines spanning 300 M instructions.  The branch-miss delta is
not: the same bytes bought -220 K, -207 K, nothing, and -146 K.**  The
function's own profile share falls every time (`c_symbol_intern` 2,89% ->
2,29% on this tree, ~145 K misses, from `-c 2003` captures reconciling with
`perf stat` to 3%), so what varies is not whether the branch stops
mispredicting but whether the program keeps the saving: predictor *capacity*
is the binding constraint, and the sibling audits landing in between kept
changing how much of it the rest of the compile wanted.  This is the
`2026-08-22a` relocation finding arriving through a different door, and it
generalizes: **a process-wide branch-miss delta is a property of the tree, not
of the change.**  Quote one only from the tree it will land on, and treat a
single-tree miss win as provisional until a second baseline reproduces it.

So the candidate's only durable effect is `+3,29 M` retired instructions
(+0,032%, losing 60 of 60 pairs across the four trees), and it stays out.  A
mask-table variant was tried too and is not cheaper: PIE address
materialization plus the index zero-extension bring it back to the same eleven
instructions plus an L1 load.  The formulation that would win on instructions
as well is an AVX-512 fault-suppressing masked byte load — no page guard
needed, since masked-off lanes do not fault — but that wants a new `simd.h`
vocabulary operation with a scalar fallback and brings the `vzeroupper` back,
which is far more than this is worth.

**The residual is genuinely irreducible.**  With the length branch removed,
`c_symbol_intern` still holds 2,29% of stage-1 misses: the empty-slot test, the
key compare and the caller's return, on a table that already answers 88,84% of
calls in one probe.  That is the survey's own premise confirmed rather than
refuted.

**Two measurement traps, both paid for here.**  `git apply --3way` *stages*
what it applies, so a later `git checkout -- <path>` restores the file from the
index and silently leaves the change in place; an entire A/B round ran against
a "baseline" that still carried the instrumentation and reported a spurious
`+117 M (+1,11%)`, and the `nm -S` size diff between the two binaries is what
exposed it.  And **pair the statistics, do not median the columns**: on
`e34f7675` the median of the base column against the median of the candidate
column says `+137 K` while the median of the fifteen per-pair differences says
`-146 K`, because pairing is what removes the between-run drift the design
exists to cancel.  Related: this repository's `.git` is shared across
worktrees, and a concurrent session popped and re-pushed stash entries mid-run
— keep candidate patches in files, not in the stash.
