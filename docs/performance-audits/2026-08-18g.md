`2026-08-18g` (Linux x86_64, Zen 4 7940HS; **make the common dense GPR
encoding a four-byte unit, and measure the population before batching**).
Serial prewarm now classifies each complete 16x16 GPR table once. When every
member is at most three architectural bytes, byte three of every record stores
its count. The hot scalar worker then copies one fixed dword and advances by
the record's authoritative architectural count. The overlapping fourth byte is
deliberately outside the logical output and is overwritten by the next
emission or remains beyond the final count; a bounded tail keeps the
exact-length copy. Longer populations retain the existing
15-byte record path. The metadata transform remains the byte authority, every
one of the 256 physical-register combinations is still checked at prewarm,
the record/table strides and BSS footprint are unchanged, and the former
reserved table byte carries the compact classification.

This is the minimal unit for the next homogeneous encoder kernel: three output
bytes plus one count, sixteen units per ZMM. A temporary real-workload census
was used to find the population rather than infer it from source. About 90.1%
of dense-register hits use compact tables. Grouping by exact `(table,
high-register)` is too sparse in bounded windows: mean occupancy is 1.58 in 64
rows and 2.49 in 256 rows, with only 24.2% of 256-row lanes in groups of eight
or more. Whole-function grouping raises the mean to 4.39 and puts 61.6% of
lanes in groups of eight or more, but would require a stable command stream and
ordered result staging. A second exact prewarm proof found that 58.0% of all
dense-register hits have a separable `base ^ low[16] ^ high[16]` encoding.
For those tables, whole-function grouping by table averages 7.59 lanes, places
79.5% of lanes in groups of eight or more, and produces full sixteen-lane
batches for 62.1% of the population. This supports a future two-`vpermd` SoA
kernel; it does not justify another arbitrary-record gather.

Two alternatives were measured and rejected. Replacing every record with only
a dword lost the longer dense-table population and regressed about 0.09%
instructions. Publishing the separable two-vector projection and consuming it
scalar remained faster than the parent, but saved only about 5.5 million
instructions versus roughly 8.2 million for the direct packed-record store;
the scalar projection work was therefore removed. Neither the census nor its
projection arrays remain in production.

Seven same-source, CPU-pinned, non-multiplexed instruction pairs are
byte-identical and reduce the median from 14,413,221,738 to 14,405,063,231,
**-8,158,507 (-0.0566%)**; every pair favors the candidate. Branches fall
2,672,640,244 -> 2,668,721,813 (**-0.1466%**) and L1d loads fall
5,175,473,387 -> 5,159,622,337 (**-0.3063%**). L1d misses fall
137,279,998 -> 135,879,906 (**-1.0199%**). Branch misses are effectively flat,
27,866,211 -> 27,874,829 (**+0.0309%**). Nine cycle pairs favor the candidate
six times; independent medians fall 5,269,254,379 -> 5,249,849,449
(**-0.3683%**).
