## Ranked leads

Ranked by the cycles they can return per line of change, with the evidence
each rests on. None is taken here; each is a byte-identical rewrite of work,
not of output, and each states its validation. Every lead is filed as an
issue (the number in its heading), so the next session can pick it up cold;
lead 4 joins the existing #447.

### 1. The FAST stack-slot liveness fixed point: 4,8% of all cycles in twelve lines (#789)

`machine_fast_close_live_ranges` (`register_allocator_fast.c:1662`) widens
slot ranges with a dense-bitset backward dataflow: `live_in = reads | (live_out
& ~writes)` per block, then a merge into every predecessor's `live_out`, swept
over *all* blocks in reverse index order until a whole sweep changes nothing.
Lines 1682–1694 and the range materialization at 1709–1723 are 4,8% of the
compile's cycles (1,24% + 1,11% + 0,76% + 0,47% + 0,35% + 0,35% + 0,24% +
0,15% + 0,11% by leaf IP); the enclosing
`machine_fast_placement_build_prepassed` is 8,9% of L1d misses and 5,9% of
branch misses, most of both on these lines. The
plane is `block_count × ceil(stack_slot_count / 64)` words and every sweep
touches all of it; the unity unit's largest frames (`max_stack_frame_bytes`
660.648, 4,85 MB of frames over 4.929 functions) are the functions where both
factors are in the thousands, and a sweep count of loop depth plus two
multiplies it. The fixed point is unique, so the result — and every byte after
it — is independent of the iteration order.

Two changes keep the result and cut the work: (a) a worklist of blocks whose
`live_out` changed, seeded with every block once, so a converged region is
never re-swept; (b) computing `live_in` only for the blocks pulled from the
worklist and merging only into the predecessors whose word actually changed
(the compare at 1694 already exists; today its result only sets a global
flag). Validation: `test_self_host` fixed point, `test_mode_matrix` for the
allocator legs, and the allocator's own regressions; the slot ranges handed to
`machine_fast_color_slots` must be identical, which the fixed point guarantees.
Expected return: most of the 4,8%; the survey's `x cycles` of 1,0 says this
loop is compute, not memory, so the saving is the iterations themselves.

### 2. `debug_variable_add_location` rescans a function's seeds once per variable: 4,4% (#790)

`debug.c:295`. The seed index groups `DebugLocationSeed`s by *function
symbol*; each variable then walks its function's whole bucket twice (count,
then copy) testing `seed->local == local` at `debug.c:312`/`328`. With L named
locals and S location seeds in one function that is 2·L·S seed reads, and the
unity unit's large functions carry both in the hundreds to thousands
(`2026-09-15T152933Z` measured 167 K seeds emitted over this workload). It is
4,1% of cycles plus 0,6% in `debug_symbol_equal`, which Clang left out of
line, 4,2% of L1d misses, and it holds the fifth and sixth lines of the flat
profile by IP (`debug.c:328` 1,10%, `debug.c:312` 0,90%).

The lead is a second `order` array keyed on `(function_symbol, local)` —
same bucket construction as `debug_location_index_build`, hash mixed over both
words — used for the exact queries `debug_add_canonical_locals` issues, while
the existing symbol-keyed index keeps serving the `IR_LOCAL_ID_INVALID`
wildcard that `debug_add_canonical_globals` issues. A stable counting sort
keeps the within-key seed order that the emitted ranges depend on, so the
DWARF is byte-identical. Validation: `-g -c` objects of the unity unit before
and after (`cmp`), `test_self_host`, the debug and DWARF regressions. Expected
return: nearly all of the 4,4%, since a variable's own seeds are a few rows.

### 3. The pipeline is module-wide between lowering and selection, and pays for it in DRAM (#791, #792)

`ir_prepare_canonical_module` validates every function, then promotes every
function, then runs FAST over every function, then publishes every function's
CFG; only then does codegen loop over functions. The average function is 274
rows of 64 bytes — 17,5 KB, an L2 working set — but every one of those passes
streams the whole module's 86 MB of rows from DRAM because the previous pass
evicted it. The survey's ranking by DRAM fills is that pipeline read
backwards:

| pass | share of DRAM fills | share of cycles | x cycles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `ir_validate_canonical_module` | 15,9% | 5,7% | 2,8 |
| `ir_fast_function` | 10,9% | 2,3% | 4,8 |
| `machine_select_canonical_function_x86_64` | 9,9% | 2,8% | 3,5 |
| `ir_cfg_publish_instruction_rows` | 6,3% | 2,1% | 3,1 |
| `ir_promote_function` | 4,5% | 1,3% | 3,5 |
| `ir_function_publish_cfg` | 4,2% | 1,1% | 4,0 |

Together 52% of the compile's 13 M DRAM fills, against an `x cycles` of 1,0
for the per-function FAST allocator that runs on rows the selector just
wrote. Within those passes, four scans exist only to compute a fact the
producer already knows, and are the cheapest part of this lead to take:

- `ir_fast_function` (`ir_fast.c:408–413`) reads every row of the function to
  sum `operand_count` and to look for `LABEL_ADDRESS`/`INDIRECT_BRANCH` before
  deciding whether the function fits the FAST budgets. That scan alone is
  6,1% of DRAM fills (lines 411–412). `IrFunction` already carries
  `opcode_summary` for exactly this question, and `ir_function_add_instruction`
  already holds `instruction.operand_count` at append time (the diagnostic
  census counts it as `OPERAND_SLOTS_APPENDED`): adding the two opcodes to
  `IR_OPCODE_SUMMARY_TRACKED` and an exact `operand_total` counter (decremented
  by the single retraction at `c_gen.c:28667`, rebuilt by `ir_rewrite_compact`,
  unknown for hand-built functions, which keep the scan) removes the pass
  without changing a budget decision.
- `machine_select_canonical_function_x86_64` (`machine_x86_64.c:7346`) scans
  every row for `IR_OPCODE_LOCAL` definitions to size the target-local
  promotion; 5,9% of DRAM fills (lines 7352–7353). The 2026-08-22 survey moved
  it from a per-value form to this row form because the per-value fetch was
  random; the per-*local* form is neither: `IR_LOCAL_PROMOTION candidates=27775`
  says the unit has 27,8 K candidate locals against 1,35 M rows, and
  `function->local_places` indexes them.
- `ir_promote_function` (`ir_promote.c:711`) scans every row for `LOCAL` and
  barrier opcodes whenever the summary admits a LOCAL row, which under direct
  frontend SSA is most functions (5,6 LOCAL rows per function on average); the
  same per-local form applies to the count, and the barrier opcodes are
  candidates for the summary.
- `ir_validate_canonical_module` proves instruction ownership by walking each
  block's `next` chain (`ir_function_instruction_owners`, `ir.c:4416`, 3,7% of
  DRAM fills and 2,6% of branch misses on its own), and
  `ir_cfg_publish_instruction_rows` (`ir_cfg.c:259`, 1,8%) walks the same
  chains to permute the rows into block order. Between the two walks FAST
  rewrites the function, so they are not the same state and `2026-09-13T130555Z`
  was right not to fuse them; but a function that FAST left unchanged (`changes
  == 0`) is the same state, and whether the validator's walk can leave the
  publication its ownership result for such a function is the least certain
  item in this list.

The larger form of the lead — running validate, promote, FAST, publish, select,
allocate and encode function by function so each function's rows stay in L2
across all of them — is the structural answer, and it points the same way as
the independent function tasks of the `2026-09-13T132324Z` architecture note.
It is not priced here beyond the fills table: the module-level steps in
between (`ir_prepare_program_abi`, the promotion statistics, the f80 cache)
would need per-function forms, and the deterministic-merge rules of
`parallelism.md` apply. The four scans above are the part that can land alone.

### 4. Direct-SSA slot table growth: 4,4% of branch misses and a memset per doubling (#447)

`c_ir_ssa_grow_slots` (`c_gen.c:4972`) doubles the per-function open-addressing
table from 64 entries, zeroing the new table and re-probing every old entry.
The `if (entry.block_plus_one)` at 4985 over a half-full table is the single
most mispredicted line of the compile (2,31 M of 52 M misses, 4,4%); together
with the probe loops at 5012–5013 (2,1%) and the finish-time slot lookups the
table is 7,7% of all branch misses; `c_ir_ssa_grow_slots` as a function is
4,9% of the LBR mispredictions (2,9% under the `branch-misses:u`
cross-check) and 0,95% of cycles, and its `memset` at 4978 is 1,6% of the
DRAM-fill samples. At finish time the builder knows
`local_count`, the block count and `event_count`, and the key population is
bounded by the events: sizing the table once from that bound removes every
rehash; keeping the high-water table across functions in the scratch arena
and clearing only its used prefix removes the memsets. Output does not depend
on table capacity. Validation: `test_self_host`, the direct-SSA regressions.

### 5. The debug-location recorder residue: about 6% (#793)

`codegen_machine_debug_index_scan` 2,0%, `codegen_machine_debug_timeline_for`
1,4%, `machine_debug_values_build` 1,2% (and 5,3% of L1d misses through
`machine_debug_block_value`), `codegen_machine_debug_index_build` 1,0%,
`codegen_record_machine_locations` 0,3%. This is what `2026-09-15T152933Z`
left after taking the recorder from 9,7 s to 3,1 s on a container and named as
the per-function index (paid once per function whatever the value count) and
the replays. Zen 5 confirms the split. The index scan and the timelines are
instruction-bound (`x cycles` 0,4 on L1d misses) and run on the machine rows
the allocator just wrote; `machine_debug_values_build` is the exception, at
4,3 L1d-miss shares per cycle share through `machine_debug_block_value`
(`machine.c:2933–2939`), which is the one line of this group worth a data
layout look before an algorithmic one.

### 6. The section bytes are copied four times: 2 M DRAM fills, about 1% (#794)

The DWARF-unwound survey names the `memcpy` call sites exactly:
`object.c:10111` copies `module->code` into the object's text section,
`driver.c:4430` copies every section of the unit's object into the invocation
arena, `link.c:1590` copies every input section into the merged object, and
`link.c:4544` copies every merged section into the ELF image. Each moves the
~40 MB of output; the four together are 8,5% of the survey's DRAM-fill
samples (2,60% + 2,44% + 2,26% + 1,17%) and most of libc's 2,4% of cycles.
`2026-08-22T091548Z` took the linker's image copy once already; the driver
copy is the odd one — for a single input it duplicates an object the unit
arena still holds — and merging a single object into the link costs a full
copy of it for nothing. The target is the `-g -c` object and the executable,
both byte-identical.

### 7. What is not a lead

- **Preprocessing and lexing.** 1,84 G instructions, 0,136 s, IPC 3,4;
  `c_lex_dispatch` 1,3%, `c_preprocess` 1,4%, `c_symbol_intern` 1,0%. The
  kernels of `2026-08-22T140940Z` and `2026-08-22T203845Z` hold on Zen 5.
- **Canonical validation as such.** `2026-09-13T130555Z` measured 2,9% of Ir
  and found the checks proportionate; this audit finds 5,7% of *cycles*
  because the walk runs on cold rows, which is lead 3's mechanism and not a
  reason to check less.
- **The linker's own work**, under 0,5% of instructions.
- **Register pressure and generated code quality.** Out of scope by
  `AGENTS.md`; stage 2's 107,7 G instructions are the price of an
  unoptimizing self-compile and are recorded as a reference, not a target.

## The TCC bootstrap was broken

The first workflow run failed in `./build.sh generate`: TCC 0.9.28rc reports
`tools/native_retirement_census.c:1672: error: assignment of read-only
location` for `settings.rows = nrc_open(...)`, and `-Werror` makes the driver
unbuildable. Bisected by inserting `settings.rows = 0;` probes: the variable
becomes read-only in TCC's view after the `string_format(...)` call that
passes the three `String8 const` archive hashes (`nrc_archived_input_sha256`
and siblings, `native_retirement_census.c:42–44`) by value through `...`;
copying them into non-const locals first compiles cleanly, and Clang 22 is
unaffected either way. That call arrived with `e099c9e2` on 2026-09-12. No CI
lane caught it because the GitHub-hosted workflows build the driver with
Clang under the documented bootstrap exception; the canonical path is only
exercised by a developer machine with TCC on `PATH` — this Arch build of TCC
reproduces it locally too. The fix is this branch's second commit (the first
adds the workflow), and the second run of the workflow bootstrapped with TCC
(`tcc version 0.9.28rc 2026-01-17`). #795 asks for a lane that guards the
bootstrap; #786 records the unrelated native-retirement closure failure that
every pull request showed at the time (the closure pinned the pre-#774
`ir.h`).

## Limits

- Sampling density: `perf record` at 4.999 Hz over a 1,15 s run is 5,9 K
  samples, so a 1% share is 59 samples; where a number is quoted to a tenth
  it comes from the survey's 59,7 K period-sampled cycles or from `perf stat`.
- No A/B: nothing here compares two compilers. The reference numbers are one
  compiler on one host, repeated five (timing) or three (counters) times with
  spreads of 0,4% on wall and 0,00% on instructions.
- The 9700X's own noise floor for paired A/B is still owned by #426; the runs
  here were serialized by the runner rather than admitted through the
  dedicated-host lease of `tools/throughput/DEDICATED.md`.
- `test_all` was not run on the runner; the branch's only source change is to
  the build driver and the GitHub matrix builds that driver with Clang.
