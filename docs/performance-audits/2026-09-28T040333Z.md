`2026-09-28T040333Z` (Linux x86_64, 4-vCPU KVM Xeon (Emerald Rapids) @ 2.1
GHz, Clang 18.1.3; **per-phase attribution of the TinyCC 0.9.27 compile-time

## Decision

No production change. This is the census asked for by
[#1605](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/1605). It charges Buster's
compile time and peak memory on identical inputs to its phases, measures each
phase per preprocessed token against TinyCC's whole compile, and names an owner
for every phase at or above 10% of the gap. Two such phases had no owner, and
this census filed one for each. It also filed owners for two smaller unowned
costs: FAST placement ([#1722](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/1722))
and `-g` location recording ([#1723](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/1723)).
The two owners filed for phases above 10% are:

- [#1700](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/1700): C-to-IR body lowering.
- [#1701](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/1701): preprocessor throughput and per-token footprint.

Findings:

1. **SQLite `-c -g0`: 0.779 s CPU and 256 MB against TinyCC's 0.165 s and
   18 MB** on main `76a7bdd`, which still declines FAST for the whole unit
   (#1601/#1602). Three phases carry the CPU gap:
   - C-to-IR lowering: 44% of the gap, 392 ns per token. It is the only phase
     whose per-token cost alone exceeds TinyCC's whole compile (241 ns per
     token).
   - Lowering-constraint validation: 23%.
   - Preprocessing: 15%.

   Codegen as a whole takes another 20%, and no single codegen pass reaches
   10%. Two phases carry 71% of the memory gap: lowering (99 MB, 41%) and
   preprocessing (71 MB, 30%).
2. **The frontend alone costs about twice TinyCC's whole compile.**
   `-fsyntax-only` takes 0.323 s CPU and peaks at 117 MB.
3. **FAST applied** (the #1601 shape spelled through a typedef) adds 6.6% CPU
   (0.788 → 0.840 s). IR preparation rises from 7% to 16% of the gap, and whole
   validation alone becomes 5.9% of the compile.
4. **Lua, 33 files in 33 processes: 1.262 s CPU against TinyCC's 0.293 s.**
   The per-process x86 table prewarm (#1295) is 68% of the CPU gap and 68% of
   the per-process memory gap. Preprocessing (15%) and lowering (12.5%) are the
   other phases above 10%.
5. **Generated code.** Buster's SQLite CLI has 718,747 instructions, TinyCC's
   401,571 and Clang `-O0`'s 282,561. The same workload runs in 1.98, 1.35 and
   1.10 s respectively.

## Attribution table

"x TinyCC" is the phase's own cost per preprocessed token divided by TinyCC's
whole-compile cost per token (241 ns on SQLite, 524 ns on Lua per-file).

"% gap" is the phase's time divided by the CPU gap: the most that removing the
phase could close. These shares sum to more than 100%, because TinyCC's own
time is not zero.

"Mem" is the peak growth charged to the phase, and "% mem gap" divides it by
the peak-RSS gap. Sampling error is roughly ±1–2 points on the larger shares.

## Limits

- No acceptance host, no A/B pair and no PMU. The sampler sees only
  `ITIMER_PROF` time, at 250 Hz, and its shares carry sampling error.
- Memory growth that happens between two samples straddling a phase boundary
  is charged to the later phase. Arenas that commit in large steps charge the
  whole step to the phase that first crosses it. The stop-point cross-check
  agrees within 1 MB.
- The Lua figures come from 1,370 samples, which is thin for the small phases.
- The SQLite version differs from the harness pin (3.53.4). The FAST row uses a
  typedef-rewritten input, not the #1678 fix.
- No sanitizer, self-host fixed-point, mode-matrix or harness gate was run. The
  compiler source was not modified.
