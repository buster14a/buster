## Which literals

A throwaway build of the base (not committed) tagged every
`c_ir_count_string_literal_range_for_target` call site in `c_parse.c`. It
logged each first-sight decode the count makes into `CStringLiteralMemo`, and
dumped the spellings decoded under the infer core.

| Call site | First-sight decodes | Spelling bytes | Escapes | Longest |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| leaf (`c_parse_string_literal_expression_type`) | 28,007 | 533,557 | 1,766 | 458 |
| infer core, whole initializer is a string | 2,936 | 11,614,769 | 68,609 | 5,538 |

- The infer core's literals are the string pools of the generated assembly
  headers (`src/buster/lib/compiler/assembly/generated/*.generated.h`).
- They are uniform, so no few very long literals dominate. The median is
  4,094 bytes, the 10th to 90th percentile is 3,902 to 4,289, and 2,425 of
  the 2,936 lie between 4 and 5 KB.
- The median literal has no escape. 64,707 of the 68,609 escapes are `\000`
  separators and 3,876 are `\"`. They are concentrated in the AArch64
  semantic pools, which have up to 1,462 escapes per literal.
- Simulating `c_ir_decode_quoted`'s window walk over the dumped spellings
  gives **223,614 window iterations**: 155,005 escape-free windows and 68,609
  that stop at an escape.

## Calls

From the base's `-fsyntax-only` profile:
- the infer core calls the count 2,529 times, for 273.3 M Ir inclusive. That
  is 2,936 fragments, because 407 come from concatenated tokens.
- `c_parse_validate_initializer_shape` calls it for the same 2,529
  initializers afterwards. Every fragment is then a memo hit, for 0.57 M.

The infer core is the first consumer of each pool and decodes each fragment
once. It makes no redundant calls. Under `-c` the count's inclusive cost is
unchanged (322.4 M), and lowering reads the recorded bytes instead of
decoding the pools again. The decode is the one `-c` reuses, so turning
it into a count under `-fsyntax-only` would only move it to lowering.

## Why a window costs what it costs

On `-march=x86-64-v3`, `BUSTER_SIMD_512` is 0. So `simd512_load_masked`,
`simd512_store_masked` and `simd512_equal_u8` are the 64-lane scalar loops in
`simd.h`, inlined into `c_ir_decode_quoted`:
- `simd.h` lines charged to `c_ir_decode_quoted` total 298.7 M of its 314.3 M
  inclusive, over all callers.
- Over the whole program, the hot fallback lines are the masked load's lane
  (136.6 M), the masked store's test and lane (100.8 M and 69.4 M) and the
  compare's lane (56.0 M).
- 272.4 M over 223,614 windows is **about 1,218 Ir per 64-byte window**, or
  23 Ir per spelling byte.

To check that against the AVX-512 path, the same kernel was copied verbatim
into a throwaway harness: `c_ir_hex_digit`, `c_ir_append_utf8`,
`c_ir_decode_escape` and `c_ir_decode_quoted`, with its census macros and
body-scratch guard removed. It decodes the 2,936 dumped spellings with Clang
18.1.3 `-O3`, and every variant produced the same 11,411,260 output bytes.

| Harness build | Ir (Callgrind) | Best of 30, wall |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `-march=x86-64-v3` | 268,635,445 | 23.49 ms |
| `-march=x86-64-v4` | (EVEX) | 1.95 ms |
| `-march=znver4` | (EVEX) | 1.27 ms |

- The v3 harness reproduces the in-compiler cost to within 1.4%.
- In the `znver4` disassembly, an escape-free window is 21 instructions: one
  `kmovq` mask, a 512-bit masked load, `vpcmpeqb`, `kortestq` and a 512-bit
  masked store.
- An escape window adds a prefix store and the scalar escape grammar, a few
  dozen instructions. That gives an estimate of about 155,005 × 21 + 68,609 ×
  50 ≈ **7 M Ir** on the AVX-512 path, against 272 M on v3. This is an
  estimate from static counts, not a measurement: Valgrind cannot run the
  EVEX build, and this container has no `perf`.
- `x86-64-v4` is slower than `znver4` because Clang 18, preferring 256-bit
  vectors there, splits each masked access in two and builds the masks with
  `vptestmq`/`kunpck` chains. That concerns a compiler binary built for
  generic v4, not the Zen tuning this audit's estimate uses.

## Reading

- The lead's cost is real on v3 builds and on every target without
  `BUSTER_SIMD_512`. On the target the compiler is tuned for, it is about 7 M
  Ir, and on this AVX-512 host 1.3 ms of kernel time over the 11.6 MB of
  pools. So about 97% of the 273 M is the scalar fallback that the
  Valgrind-compatible build selects, not decode work.
- Nothing about the pools' shape calls for a different kernel: 69% of the
  windows are escape-free full 64-byte copies. The infer core makes no excess
  calls, and the decode must stay because `-c` reuses it.
- No candidate was built. Changing the kernel for Zen 5 cannot recover more
  than a fraction of about 7 M Ir, and these hosted v3 counts cannot measure
  it. Changing the `simd.h` fallback would cut the hosted counts by about
  265 M with no effect on the Zen 5 path. It would also move every hosted
  Callgrind baseline that runs a masked kernel.
- For hosted censuses on v3 builds: any kernel built on
  `simd512_load_masked`/`simd512_store_masked` is overstated by about two
  orders of magnitude against its AVX-512 cost. A census lead in such a
  kernel should be checked against the `BUSTER_SIMD_512` path before work
  starts. The #3503 candidate's first failure (the masked-load fallback on
  v3) is another instance.
- Performance validation is incomplete (#2761). Hosted wall time is
  diagnostic, and no benchmark request was made.
