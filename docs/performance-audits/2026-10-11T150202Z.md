## Workload

Generated C, one translation unit per cell, compiled by
`ide cc [-v] -g0 -c -target {x86_64-unknown-linux | x86_64-windows-msvc} gG.uU.c`.
The generator:

```python
# G constructor priority groups (one entry each, priorities 101..), one
# unprioritized constructor tail, and U unrelated data relocations.
o = ["int sink;", "static void target(void) { sink += 1; }"]
for g in range(G):
    o.append(f"__attribute__((constructor({101 + g}))) static void c{g}(void) {{ sink += {g + 1}; }}")
o.append("__attribute__((constructor)) static void tail(void) { sink += 7; }")
if U:
    o.append(f"void (*table[{U}])(void) = {{" + ",".join(["target"] * U) + "};")
o.append("int main(void) { return sink == 0; }")
```

G ∈ {0, 1, 8, 64, 512} and U ∈ {0, 1,000, 10,000, 100,000} vary independently,
in a full cross for both formats (40 cells). G = 0 is the no-group control
(COFF still renames to `.CRT$XCU`/`.CRT$XTX`); G = 1 is the small-group
control. Initializer entries are G + 1. R and the relocations in initializer
sections (`.init_array*`, `.fini_array*`, `.CRT$X*`) are counted with
`objdump -r`. `relocation_visits` is the `OBJECT_WRITE` ledger from `ide cc -v`.
Instructions are Callgrind (`--cache-sim=no --branch-sim=no`) inclusive Ir:
the splitter from the census binary, the writer (`object_write_core`) and the
whole compile from the stock binary. Wall time is 15 runs of the stock binary
per cell, all 40 cells interleaved per round after one discarded warmup
round, unpinned, on a 4-vCPU shared VM.

The last column is the visit count the pre-repair splitter
(`3fe508f01^1`, before [PR #1518](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/pull/1518))
would have made by its own loops: one sweep of all R per group plus one
remainder sweep of R per array that had a group, so G·R + R here (only the
init array has groups). It is **derived from that source**, not measured.

## Findings

- **The multiplicative term is gone.** The ELF ledger reads exactly `3R` in
  all 20 cells, whatever G is, and the COFF ledger reads `3R + 2·S'·R` with
  G = 0, plus `R` once a group exists. For G = 512, R = 101,543 the pre-repair
  splitter would have made 52.1 M visits by its own loops; the repaired one
  makes none on ELF and one placed copy (R) on COFF.
- **What the splitter still costs is at most 0.69% of the compile's
  instructions in every cell** (largest: 645,780 of 94,799,539 Ir, 0.681%, at
  ELF G = 512, U = 0). It has two parts:
  - an O(G) table build, which is 92% section-name formatting
    (`string_format`, 1,157 Ir per group). The map itself is about 103 Ir per
    group, 52,986 Ir self at G = 512;
  - on COFF with a group, the placed copy, about 29 Ir per relocation
    (2.90 M Ir at R = 100,018), which is 3.4% of the COFF writer and 0.30% of
    the compile at G = 1.
- **ELF pays for placement even when there are no groups.** At G = 0 the stock
  writer spends 17.0 Ir per relocation on `object_initializer_relocation_place`
  (no-place counterfactual: 1,700,285 Ir of 18,611,448 at R = 100,007, which
  is 9.1% of the ELF writer and 0.19% of the compile; 17,285 Ir at R = 1,007).
  The objects are byte-identical. #990 asked that the no-group path not
  acquire new per-relocation work; this is not a new pass, but it is a
  per-relocation check that the no-split view did not have. Hoisting one
  "anything grouped" test out of the plan and emit loops would remove it. It
  is recorded on #990 as an open, unmeasured candidate, not a claim.
- **The large residual is #3407's, and priority groups multiply it.** Each
  group is one more COFF section, so the `2·S'·R` term grows with G. At
  G = 512, U = 100,000 the COFF ledger is 110.4 M visits, the COFF writer is
  1,346 M Ir (59.6% of the compile's 2,260 M), and the hosted wall median is
  374 ms, against 170 ms for the same source on ELF (20.4 M Ir writer). At
  G = 0 with the same U the COFF writer is 79.4 M Ir. This is attributed to
  #3407 and not to #990.
- Wall time follows R and the COFF residual. Within this VM's noise
  (max/min up to 2× in a cell), nothing in the splitter is visible in the
  wall time.

## Decision on a 9700X measurement

**Not requested, and no workload prepared.** The repair is already on `main`.
Its removed cost is a derived visit count. Its remaining splitter cost is
≤ 0.69% of compile Ir at an extreme G = 512, and ≤ 0.31% at G ≤ 8. A paired
physical A/B would need a pre-repair baseline, and the nearest one
(`3fe508f01^1`) differs by the whole #1518 object-emission redesign, so it
could not attribute a difference to the remap. A same-source counterfactual
that reinstates the G·R loop would measure a synthetic, unshipped
compiler. Neither result could change a decision here, so the evidence does
not justify spending the host. The #3407 residual is the one with a
measurable effect, and it belongs to that issue's own experiment.

## Verdict

**Neutral, scoped.** The G·R splitter term is removed (exact ledger counts on
both formats; 52.1 M derived visits avoided at G = 512, R ≈ 100k). Its
remaining cost is O(G + R) and bounded at ≤ 0.69% of compile instructions on
these inputs. No compiler-wide or wall-time speedup is claimed, and none is
validated on the 9700X.

## Validation and limits

- Callgrind counts instructions, not time, on `-march=x86-64-v3` builds whose
  AVX-512 kernels are compiled out; the census binary adds call overhead to
  the three functions it keeps out of line.
- Generated single-TU inputs with one entry per priority group and only
  constructors (no destructors); real programs have far fewer groups. One
  host, unpinned, no `perf` counters; wall times are hosted diagnostics.
- Not run: the 9700X A/A and A/B, sanitizer, self-host fixed point and test
  suites (no code was changed).
