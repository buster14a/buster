# Native differential worker qualification for #408

## Result and rollout boundary

Requesting four bounded differential workers is the qualified production
budget for all four hosted Unix native lanes. The existing runner still clamps
that request by logical CPU count, `BUSTER_TEST_JOBS`, and
`BUSTER_SINGLE_THREADED`; the screened macOS AArch64 runner therefore used
three effective workers. The proposed production change is only `--jobs 4` on
the ordinary native full-corpus invocation. The self-test is unchanged, and an
omitted `--jobs` or explicit `--jobs 1` remains the one-worker reproduction
path.

Across the four platforms, the sum of the per-platform median full-corpus wall
times fell from 2945.591 seconds at requested one to 1154.177 seconds at
requested four, a 60.8% reduction. Requested four was also 27.8% below
requested two. Every individual platform improved. The longest measured
corpus, macOS x86_64, fell from a 1443.981-second median at one worker to
484.759 seconds at four, a 66.4% reduction.

These are full-corpus invocation measurements. They are **not** complete native
job or workflow measurements, and the percentages must not be reported as
full-job or end-to-end CI speedups. Three exact-head complete native-job
attempts remain required after integration.

No production source, compiler configuration, corpus, sanitizer policy,
reference boundary, reducer, seed, timeout, native object, or upload policy is
changed. This work does not touch #36 or use the #437 persistent benchmark
host.

Run 34808489519 is recorded as failed because the original comparator did not
normalize row zero in headerless per-case `processes.tsv` files and retained
Apple linker diagnostics contained the exact fresh output root. These were
comparison-harness defects, not corpus failures: all 36 full-corpus invocations
exited zero, cleanup passed, and all semantic evidence was retained. The fixed
comparator was regression-tested and replayed on every artifact as described
above. The failed run is not relabeled green, and no rerun was dispatched merely
to replace the badge.

Requested one was rejected as the production policy because it left 50.5% to
66.4% of measured per-platform corpus wall time removable. Requested two was a
valid fallback but was not selected: requested four reduced every platform
again, including 17.6% on the least-responsive Linux x86_64 cohort and 33.9% on
the critical Intel macOS cohort. Four remained bounded by the existing CPU,
quota, and single-threaded clamps. No unbounded or nested compiler concurrency
was introduced.
