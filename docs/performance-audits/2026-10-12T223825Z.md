`2026-10-12T223825Z` (Linux x86_64, hosted Intel Xeon @ 2.80GHz (4 vCPU),
callgrind on x86-64-v3 builds; **x86-64 encoder census after #3871 and lazy
dense GPR table cells (#124)**).

## Scope

This audit records the census and results behind two
[#124](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/issues/124) slices:
[PR #3871](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/pull/3871) (pointer `MOVUPS`
copy chunks and `LEA_FRAME` rematerializations through prepared rows, merged)
and [PR #3927](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/pull/3927) (dense GPR
table cells encoded on first read). It also records one rejected experiment.
Every number is a callgrind instruction count, reported by the change's
author from a hosted container. None of it is timing, Zen 5 evidence or
acceptance evidence; #124's 9700X acceptance stays open.

## Method

- **Builds:** `./build.sh generate -DBUSTER_NATIVE_TARGET=x86-64-v3`, then
  `./build.sh build --config Release -t ide`. The driver was bootstrapped
  with the host Clang, not TinyCC, because the container has no TinyCC.
  Baseline and candidate were built one after the other in the same path.
- **Profiler:** `valgrind --tool=callgrind --cache-sim=no --branch-sim=no`.
- **Self-compile workload:** a frozen `git archive` copy of the base's
  `src/` plus its `build/generated`, compiled with
  `ide cc -Isrc -Ibuild/generated -DBUSTER_UNITY_BUILD=1 -DBUSTER_INCLUDE_TESTS=0 -g -march=znver3 src/buster/apps/ide/ide.c -o OUT -lm`.
  Baseline and candidate compiled the same frozen tree.
- **Small workloads:** `ide cc -c tests/basic_c_operations.c`, and a
  one-function file `int f(int a, int b) { return a * b + 3; }`.
- **Output identity:** every compared pair of outputs was checked with `cmp`.

## Census on main `b7354cef` (after #3871)

The self-compile counted 27,322,821,431 Ir. Of that,
`machine_encode_x86_64_into` was 871.9 M inclusive:

- 404 M was its own self cost, about 172 Ir per MIR row over 2.35 M rows.
- `machine_x64_emit_exact_recipe` was 237 M over 1.81 M calls.
- `machine_x64_flags_dead_at` was 102.7 M over 178 K calls. Nearly all of
  that is the lazy whole-function flag-liveness solve.

Hot-path metadata calls were 22.1 M inclusive, mostly first-use shape misses.
That puts the bridge under 0.15% of the self-compile.

In `tests/basic_c_operations.c -c` (64.04 M), the x86 encoder was 34.5% of
the run, almost all of it first-use preparation:

- `machine_x64_gpr_encoding_table_fill` was 8.86 M: 45 whole tables and
  7,334 metadata encodes.
- `machine_x64_fixed_template_resolve` was 8.6 M.
- Mnemonic-bridge shape resolution was about 12 M over 32 resolutions.
  Each resolution normalized about 42 candidate forms, about 7.5 K Ir per
  form on first touch.

## PR #3927 result

All outputs are byte-identical to the base.

| Workload | Base `b7354cef` | Candidate | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| one-function `-c` | 13,635,810 | 12,660,017 | −7.2% |
| `tests/basic_c_operations.c -c` | 64,042,618 | 55,730,631 | −13.0% |
| stage-1 self-compile | 27,322,821,431 | 27,311,600,241 | −0.04% |

In the `basic_c_operations.c` run, dense-table preparation fell from 8.86 M
to 0.54 M, and `buster_x86_metadata_emit_exact_machine` fell from 8.70 M to
1.40 M. The self-compile is neutral as expected, because the gang prewarm
still fills every cell.

## Rejected experiment

On `b7354cef`, two changes were tried together:

- keeping the next edit point in a local in `machine_encode_x86_64_into`;
- answering `machine_x64_flags_dead_at` with an in-block scan of at most
  sixteen rows, falling back to the solve.

The output stayed identical, but the self-compile rose from 27,322,821,431
to 27,378,550,783 Ir (+56 M). `machine_x64_flags_dead_at` rose from
104.8 M to 158.5 M, because the scan rarely avoids the whole-function solve
and adds its own cost. The edit-point local showed no measurable change. The
experiment was reverted and is not part of either PR.

## Remaining leads

- First-use shape resolution through `buster_x86_metadata_select_form`. A
  candidate pre-filter must keep the diagnostics for invalid source assembly,
  which depend on candidate order.
- The 9700X time-to-artifact measurement that #124 requires.
