## Method

The method is the frozen `link-tls-v1` profile (`python3 -I
tools/link_tls_bench.py --profile`). The route builds the inputs on the host
with clang. It then links every cell once with each binary and requires the
two images to be byte-identical and to print the frozen output. Only after
that does it time the cell. Each cell gets 2 warmups, then 20 alternating AB
and BA pairs of fresh `taskset -c 2 ide cc ... -o OUT` processes. Each
process's wall time is measured from spawn to `wait4`, and its user plus
system CPU time comes from the same `wait4`. The statistic is the median
paired ratio with a distribution-free 95% order-statistic interval.
Inputs are warm in the page cache, so nothing here describes a cold link.

`m<N>.o` holds N `__thread long` variables with two general-dynamic sites
each, so it has T=2N TLSGD/`__tls_get_addr` pairs in one `.text` section.
`n16384.o` uses `-fno-plt`. `m16384.a` is a one-member archive of
`m16384.o`. `plain.o` is the no-TLS control: 4096 globals and 4096
functions.

## Results

The ratio below is scan wall time divided by index wall time. "Index faster"
counts the pairs in which the index link finished first.

| cell | T | index median ms | scan median ms | scan/index [95% interval] | index faster |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| tls-gd-2048 | 2,048 | 3.410 | 12.536 | 3.652 [3.599, 3.970] | 20/20 |
| tls-gd-8192 | 8,192 | 4.919 | 151.961 | 31.49 [30.40, 33.07] | 20/20 |
| tls-gd-32768 | 32,768 | 10.017 | 2,392.108 | 238.96 [236.15, 243.46] | 20/20 |
| tls-gd-32768-pie | 32,768 | 9.689 | 1,601.855 | 165.44 [164.03, 169.95] | 20/20 |
| tls-gd-32768-no-plt | 32,768 | 9.990 | 2,401.317 | 240.56 [236.58, 249.96] | 20/20 |
| tls-gd-32768-archive | 32,768 | 11.100 | 2,397.515 | 216.34 [211.47, 217.40] | 20/20 |
| no-tls-control | 0 | 6.783 | 6.776 | 1.004 [0.948, 1.018] | 12/20 |

Scan medians grow 12.1x from T=2,048 to T=8,192 and 15.7x from T=8,192 to
T=32,768. The second step approaches the 16x that a pure T² term gives.
Index medians grow 1.44x and 2.04x over the same steps. Median CPU time
tracks wall time in every cell; for example, the T=32,768 scan cell used
2,389.9 ms of CPU against 2,392.1 ms of wall. The scan cost is therefore
computation, not I/O or scheduling. The no-TLS control interval includes 1.0,
so this experiment shows neither a speedup nor a regression for ordinary
links.

The route's other legs ran on the same pair, and none of them involves the
linker change. The `compiler-compare-v1` self-host compile showed no
detectable difference: wall B/A 0.9998, 95% CI [0.9994, 1.0005], 186 pairs.
The throughput corpus had 0 confirmed regressions in 12 cases. The inline
acceptance leg measures `-fcanonical-inline` on the candidate alone and does
not bear on #1915.

## Interpretation and limits

- The experiment replaces the index's membership query with a reconstructed
  scan. The index setup and #2247's publication guards stay the same in both
  arms: the scan arm still builds the sparse index, and it also stores and
  assigns the `ObjectFile*` the scan needs. The ratios therefore measure the
  query change against an otherwise constant link. They do not measure a
  pre-#2247 linker that skips the index build. The hosted replay
  `2026-10-12T121724Z` compared whole revisions, so it could not separate
  the query from the guards. No A/A rebuild control was run. Still, the
  measured effect exceeds 160x in every 32,768-site cell, far beyond any
  plausible build-layout effect.
- The restored scan was written for this experiment; it is not the literal
  pre-#2247 revision. Its images were byte-identical to the index's in all
  7 cells, and it passed the same local replay, but it is still a
  reconstruction.
- On the 9700X, the index links T=32,768 in about 10 ms. The hosted replay
  measured 35 ms there, and the scan took 2,712 ms hosted versus 2,392 ms
  here.
- This is matched Zen 5 evidence for warm, single-object or one-member
  archive general-dynamic links only. It does not cover cold cache, many
  small objects, large multi-member archives or a phase breakdown. Those
  #1915 cohorts remain pending.
- Self-host and the throughput corpus contain little or no general-dynamic
  TLS, so no compiler-throughput change is claimed.
