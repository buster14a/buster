## Decision and scope

**No-go for changing the two-worker default under the predeclared qualification
rule.** Four workers produce a real isolated wall-time reduction, but one of the
four order-balanced pairs misses the 10% minimum, and every pair increases
waited-descendant CPU time by far more than the 5% allowance. Preserve the
ordinary eight-shard/two-worker policy, one-worker reproduction, indivisible unity
TU, every existing checker, supported build and fail-closed aggregate. No shard
assignment or required-check inventory changes. #2033 owns this conclusion;
#2032 owns duplicate-reference removal and #2030 owns unity-only scope research.
Neither depends on accepting four workers.

This decision is **under an explicit experiment envelope**, not proof that four
workers are unsafe or never worthwhile. The CPU allowance is a predeclared
acceptance choice, not an existing Buster admission contract. CPU seconds are
resource occupancy, not retired instructions or a count of new algorithmic work.
A maintainer who prioritizes isolated analyzer latency over that CPU allowance
could choose a different tradeoff in a separately predeclared campaign.

The predeclared rule requires **every** pair to improve wall by at least 10%,
increase child CPU by at most 5%, retain exact successful coverage/diagnostics,
and have sufficient CPU/memory headroom. The wall and CPU requirements fail.
These fixed-count observations are not confidence bounds or a universal claim
about every future runner image or workload.

Trial 1 reports AMD EPYC 7763; trial 2 reports AMD EPYC 9V74. Both guests report
four logical CPUs with **two cores and two threads per core**, affinity `0-3`,
and roughly 15.609 GiB guest memory. The recorded service/system cgroup ancestors
have unlimited CPU/memory quotas and cpuset `0-3`; the root has unavailable
controller-specific limit files, not an invented numeric zero. This is guest
exposure, not proof of dedicated underlying physical-core allocation.

The configured worker peak is 2/4 and sampled live-process peak 5/9. Memory has
large observed headroom: the largest sampled summed RSS is 1,261,760,512 bytes,
about 7.53% of guest MemTotal, below the 25% qualification ceiling. No recorded
service/system cgroup OOM/high/max event increased, swap remained zero in those
cgroups, and whole-host SwapFree was unchanged. Job cgroup memory peaks for the
entire campaign ended at 1,542,848,512 / 1,581,748,224 bytes; these include the
runner, bootstrap, controls and file cache and are not per-arm RSS. Host memory
pressure totals moved by only 1-2 microseconds; those snapshots cannot exclude
brief pressure between samples. Memory failure is not the reason for rejection.

**Inference, not an executed causal experiment:** the exposed SMT topology and
larger concurrent CPU-time cost are consistent with resource contention when
four heavy analyzers use two guest cores. No PMU, sibling-affinity intervention
or private-hypervisor measurement was run, so SMT is not established as the
sole cause and extra CPU seconds are not extra analyzer instructions.

## Runner occupancy, whole-CI impact and the straggler

Mean two/four-worker arm wall is 638.160161 / 575.759278 seconds on trial 1 and
601.420525 / 523.049904 seconds on trial 2. A **single candidate inventory**
therefore saves 62.400883 / 78.370621 VM-occupancy seconds in this diagnostic
comparison, while mean child CPU time rises 54.130% / 43.639%. VM occupancy and
summed child CPU time are distinct: reserved-runner billing is not proportional
to these CPU-time counters. There is no measured production throughput gain,
billing calculation or new ordinary-CI four-worker run.

For a separately anchored whole-CI replay, source
`af6b2b100acfdd9c970e35e33d4468cd35883812`, successful merge-group
[run 36759465982](https://github.com/buster14a/buster/actions/runs/36759465982),
has 11,907 summed job-execution seconds (198m27s of VM occupancy, excluding
queue time and billing rounding/multipliers). Its analyzer finished at
18:55:31Z, Windows AArch64 release at 18:55:20Z, and the final aggregate at
18:55:47Z. Holding every other job, start and aggregate overhead fixed, **any**
analyzer saving greater than 11 seconds advances that run's final prerequisite
by only 11 seconds; Windows becomes the bottleneck. This is a counterfactual
replay, not an executed end-to-end speedup. Applying the new diagnostic's
62-78 second candidate-only saving to that different source is not acceptance
measurement; conditionally it represents roughly 0.52-0.66% of the historical
summed job occupancy. #2032's independently observed removable 616-second
reference pass remains separate and must not be attributed to four workers.

Four-worker arms expose shard 2 as a clear straggler: shard elapsed is
562.884137 / 588.149653 / 509.610213 / 536.089915 seconds, within 0.26 seconds
of the corresponding complete run. Its expensive units include `c_test.c`
(frontend tests, unit 13) and `c_parse.c` (unit 65), with `c_source.c` also in the
same shard. For example, trial 2 sample 1 spends 228.817282 / 128.816711 seconds
on `c_test.c` / `c_parse.c`. Those are shard/TU wall times, not CPU attribution.
A duration-aware deterministic grouping could shorten this tail under fixed
costs, but costs visibly change with the worker budget. No grouping prototype,
speedup claim or production reassignment was made; it cannot by itself prove
that the CPU-time qualification rule will pass.

## Validation, limits and smallest useful implementation

The smallest retained implementation is the native opt-in qualification option,
manual hosted workflow and independent reader/regressions, plus this no-change
result. The temporary branch-only push trigger is removed. Ordinary default
workers, checker flags, source/test assignment and CI admission stay unchanged.
Rollback removes that option/workflow/reader/test; `--jobs 1` and normal analysis
keep their existing path. A future latency-priority decision must predeclare its
CPU/resource tradeoff and workload/runner envelope before rerunning.

benchmarking/research/workflow/tracking guidance and the newest audit; searches

**Strongest counterargument:** four workers really reduce isolated VM occupancy
and could be preferable on a future critical path or a runner with more guest
cores, even with greater summed CPU occupancy. The present conservative envelope
and near-coincident Windows bottleneck do not establish that four workers are
intrinsically harmful. They justify preserving the current default for this
campaign, not banning explicit `--jobs 4`.
