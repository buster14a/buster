`2026-08-22d` (Linux x86_64, Zen 4 7940HS; **hotspot 6 of the 2026-08-21
stage-1 branch-miss survey: punctuator ladders, and the two predicates hiding
behind them**).  The survey put ~1.2% of all stage-1 branch misses in serial
`c_token_is_punctuator` ladders — four or five chained compares per token in
`c_ir_unsupported_gnu_construct`, `c_ir_matching_delimiter`,
`c_ir_lower_frame_fallback`'s inlined copies, `c_ir_emit_prepared_call_step`
and `c_parse_label_address_prefix` — and proposed replacing each with one
classification of `token.punctuator`.  Developed on the `b2c52677` tree
concurrently with `2026-08-22a`-`c`, so every per-site increment below is
against that tree; the reference points at the end of this entry are
re-measured on the merged tree and are the ones the next audit starts from.
`C_PUNCTUATOR_COUNT` is 57, so the whole enum fits one `u64`: `c_punctuator_in_set` (header-inline in `c.h`, beside
`c_token_length` and for the same reason) answers a set membership with a shift
and a mask, `C_PUNCTUATOR_BIT` builds the sets, and a new
`BUSTER_CT_CHECK(C_PUNCTUATOR_COUNT <= 64)` fails the build rather than let a
65th punctuator silently truncate one.  `C_PUNCTUATOR_NONE` is bit 0, so a set
that leaves bit 0 clear keeps the invariant the sites rely on — only a
`C_TOKEN_PUNCTUATOR` token carries an id — and still needs no kind test.

**Most of the ladders were already free, and converting them costs
instructions.**  The `CPunctuator` enum orders AMPERSAND, STAR, PLUS, MINUS,
TILDE and EXCLAMATION at ids 37-42 and PLUS_PLUS/MINUS_MINUS at 6-7, so clang
compiles the eight-way prefix-operator chain in `c_ir_unary_expression_end` to
`lea -0x25(%r8),%r9d; cmp $0x6,%r9d; jb` plus `add $-6,%r8d; cmp $0x1,%r8d;
jbe` — four instructions and two branches, cheaper than a shift-and-mask.  Six
such sites in `c_ir_unary_expression_end` and `c_ir_lower_expression_core_step`
were converted, measured at **+4.6 M instructions**, and reverted.  A mask pays
only where clang cannot fold the chain: **runtime comparands**, as in
`c_ir_matching_delimiter`/`c_parse_matching_delimiter`, where
`C_PUNCTUATOR_BIT(opening) | C_PUNCTUATOR_BIT(closing)` hoists out of the scan
(**-6.0 M**); and **arms with side effects**, as in
`c_ir_unsupported_gnu_construct`'s four bracket counters, which now sit behind
one set test (**-4.2 M**) — the branchless `+=`/`-=` form of the same counters
was tried first and measured **+27.2 M**, exactly the `2026-08-08i` trap of
adding per-token work in the name of removing branches.  `c_parse_ast` and
`c_parser_declarator_list_segment_end` classify their three-way open/close
delimiter sets the same way (**-0.5 M** instructions but **-3.6 M** branches;
the two sides interleave in the enum, so neither folds — the same
non-folding shape `2026-08-22c` left open-coded in
`c_ir_build_delimiter_index`, which these macros now cover and which is
worth its own measurement).  The two backward
control-head scans in `c_ir_named_label_at` and
`c_parse_label_address_prefix_proven` were converted for consistency and are
flat (**-0.1 M**, within noise).

**The cost at those sites was not the ladders.**  A cycle profile put
`c_parse_label_address_prefix` at 1.00% and `c_ir_named_label_at` at 1.02% of
stage-1 cycles — two whole predicates called *out of line*, the first once per
token scanned by `c_ir_unsupported_gnu_construct`, the second twice per body
token by the two loops that size and fill a body's label table, both answering
no almost every time.  Each keeps its full proof out of line
(`c_parse_label_address_prefix_proven`, `c_ir_named_label_proven_at`) and gains
a header-inline necessary condition: the `&&` a label address is spelled with,
and an identifier followed by a colon, with the range tests that make the two
token reads in bounds ahead of them.  That alone is **-288 M** of the total.
Marking the two cold proofs `BUSTER_COLD BUSTER_PRESERVE_MOST` — the attribute
`c_ir_label_colon_at` next door already carries — measured **+6.1 M** and was
reverted; the callee-side saves outweigh the caller-side savings at this call
frequency.
