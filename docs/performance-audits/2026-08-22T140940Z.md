`2026-08-22T140940Z` (Linux x86_64, Zen 4 7940HS; **SIMD-first lexing
evaluated against the landed compaction emitter: a census of the stage-1
workload, the window pipeline's lane utilization, and 64-byte-stride escape
scans (stage 1 -37,0 M instructions)**).
A fresh evaluation of SIMD-first lexing for the C frontend, starting — per
the AGENTS.md rule that data layout and measured shape come before
instructions — from a character-class census of the real workload rather
than from the kernel.  The Validark-method window pipeline itself has been
the default lexer since `2026-08-10b` (`c_lex_compact`, `-102,3 M` then),
so the question this entry answers is what "SIMD-first" still has left to
claim.  The census's answer was unambiguous: not the windows — the escape.

**The census, probe-counted over the unity stage-1 compile** (temporary
counters in `c_lex_compact`, `c_lex_scan_one` and `c_lex_dispatch` plus a
256-entry byte histogram, patch kept out of every A/B binary).  27.811.251
bytes reach the lexer across 26.153 dispatches — 326 of them files (the
`-fsource-metrics` view), the rest macro replacement texts, pastes and
builtin spellings re-lexed as small buffers.  By byte class: alpha 63,31%,
space 13,60%, digit 11,02%, punctuator bytes 7,55%, underscore 2,23%,
newline 1,16%, backslash 0,80%, quotes 0,19%; the single byte `A` is
19,01% of the entire workload and `,` is 2,28%, both signatures of the
generated tables.  By span (the `-fsource-metrics` aggregate): **literal
bytes are 48,0% of everything lexed** (13,15 MB of 27,37 MB), whitespace
13,8%, comments 3,5%.  By token: punctuators 49,5%, identifiers 19,9%,
preprocessing numbers 19,7%, newlines 10,6%, literals 0,3% — half the
token stream is single- and double-byte punctuation, and the giant
literals that dominate the byte count are 0,3% of tokens.

**Lane utilization of the window pipeline.**  283.569 windows advance
13.963.372 bytes — a mean of 49,2 of the 64 loaded lanes per window
(76,9%), 11,0 tokens per emitting window, 2,72% of windows advancing zero
bytes.  76% of emitting windows carry 15 or fewer tokens, so the 8-row
interleaved stores waste 27,1% of their row-store lanes on tail slack
(4.122.992 rows stored for 3.006.795 tokens).  The forward-seeking cursor
runs 2,35 iterations per window, and 93,1% of those iterations are
preprocessing numbers (620.370 of 666.376) — numbers are the one item
class whose extent the cursor resolves serially on every sighting, where
comments and literals amortize over their spans.  Window defers are 21,3%
of windows (28.140 numbers touching the window edge, 17.777 comments,
14.414 literals).  And the decisive number: **the scalar escape
`c_lex_scan_one` consumed 13.847.879 bytes — 49,8% of the entire
workload — through its byte loops**: 13,07 MB of literal bodies at a mean
of 907 bytes per literal (the generated string tables), 465,7 KB of line
comments, 311,6 KB of block comments.  The window pipeline, for all its
width, was lexing half the bytes; every one of those long items was walked
by the 8-byte table-AND run loops, and every backslash in the tables (one
per ~58 literal bytes) broke the run and bounced through the outer loop.

**What was built: 64-byte-stride finds for the three escape shapes.**
`c_lex_scan_one` keeps its structure — and its exact stop-byte handling,
diagnostics and metrics attribution — but when the caller is the window
pipeline (a new `CLexState.simd_scan` flag, set only on the compact
dispatch) the plain runs between stop bytes fast-forward by full 512-bit
chunks: line comments seek the newline with one compare per 64 bytes,
block comments seek the next newline or star-slash pair (the pair's
lookahead load masked by the file bounds, exactly as the window loop's
lookaheads are), and literal bodies seek the first of this literal's own
delimiter, backslash, or newline.  Two details are the design: the
literal find seeks *this* literal's delimiter where the shared
`c_literal_plain_table` also stops at the other quote kind, so a
apostrophe-dense string literal no longer stops at all; and escape pairs
are consumed by the unchanged outer loop rather than by in-chunk parity,
so no escape state crosses a chunk and the simdjson parity algorithm
stays confined to the window pipeline.  The reference path
(`c_lex_reference`, and every host without the emitter) keeps the byte
loops, which converts the differential gate from sharing one hard-case
implementation into genuinely comparing two — the construct cases slid
across window boundaries, the whole-file corpus at every phase, and the
fuzz blobs now exercise the stride scans against the scalar loops.

- **Adjudicated for the record: the lexer still cannot write the
  preprocessor's final slots.**  `2026-08-22T120206Z` settled that
  finality first exists in the preprocessor's line loop — newlines,
  directives and inactive regions are consumed, expansion rewrites the
  rest — so a "SIMD lexer writes the final token array" design stays
  closed.  What the window emitter does write is the same 12-byte
  `CToken` row format those final slots hold, eight rows per two stores,
  and the preprocessor's straight-through majority (64% of output tokens)
  copies them once into the `CTokenStream` arena.  That copy is the
  preprocessor's cost, not the lexer's, and it is already the achievable
  form.
- **Measured and left standing, ranked by the census:** (1) the cursor's
  620 K serial number-extent iterations — 93% of cursor work — look
  replaceable by carry-propagation mask arithmetic (`ends = (continue +
  seeds) & ~continue`, spans by `ends - seeds`), but at ~15 instructions
  per iteration the whole slice is ~10 M and the interaction with
  comment/literal precedence needs the cursor kept for the rare classes;
  (2) the 8-row store tail waste (27,1% of row-store lanes) and the
  `punctuators` array round-trip (`2026-08-10b`'s five-blend lead) are
  both bounded by the ~1,9 M store passes — single-digit M each; (3)
  window re-classification overlap is 23% of loaded lanes (49,2 of 64
  advanced), inherent to item-aligned windows.  None of these clears the
  bar the escape scan just met; they are recorded so the next session
  ranks against them instead of re-deriving them.
- **One census oddity worth a note:** `defer_literal` and the literal
  escape count are both exactly 14.414 — the counters say no literal ever
  deferred mid-window and then resolved in the next window, where
  straddling short literals should produce some.  The token counts
  cross-check against `-fsource-metrics` exactly, so the equality is a
  property of how defers re-window (the second sighting lands at lane 0
  and escapes without a second defer count), not a miscount; the census
  probe patch is preserved in the audit trail for whoever wants the
  distribution.
- **Do not retry:** in-chunk escape parity for the literal find.  The
  outer loop's per-backslash stop costs ~20 instructions per ~58-byte
  escape gap on this corpus (~4,5 M total); resolving pairs inside the
  chunk would save part of that in exchange for parity state carried
  across chunk reloads — the exact cross-boundary state class the
  `2026-08-10b` architecture exists to exclude.  The remaining headroom
  is low-single-digit M against a real bug surface.
