## What the instrument changes, which is most of the value of this round

1. **Callgrind counts `rep stosb` at one Ir a byte.** Measured directly on this
   host: a `memset` of 8 KB costs **8.229 Ir**, of 64 KB **65.572 Ir**, of 512
   bytes 62 and of 64 bytes 31 — glibc's `__memset_avx2_unaligned_erms` takes
   the ERMS path above about two kilobytes, and VEX unrolls it. On the machine
   that same `rep stosb` retires *one* instruction and costs only bandwidth.
   So a libc fill's Ir number **is the bytes it moved**, and the metric this
   repository trends is blind to exactly the class of waste the previous audit
   had to price at "~0 M" and defer ("the largest memory-traffic item in the
   compiler ... needs a quiet machine"). It does not need a quiet machine. It
   needs an instrument that counts bytes, and this is one.
2. **Valgrind 3.22 cannot decode EVEX**, so the measured binary is
   `-march=x86-64-v3` and every AVX-512 kernel in the tree — the lex window's
   `vpermi2b` class table, the 64-byte string-literal decode, the preprocessor's
   class-mask projection, the identifier mask over the shape sidecar — is
   compiled out of it and appears as its scalar fallback. That is why this
   tree's Ir total (9.583 G) sits above the previous round's `instructions:u`
   (7.902 G) on the same source: the difference is mostly `simd.h`'s fallbacks,
   which measure 725 M here. **Deltas from architecture-neutral work transfer;
   an AVX-512 item has to be priced by census instead**, and one below is.
3. **There is no skid and no sampling alias.** Lessons 3 and 4 of the previous
   round — cross-check a symbol at two sampling periods, line attribution
   inside a hot function overstates by ~2x — do not apply to a counting
   instrument. Every line number in this entry is exact. Lesson 6 still holds:
   inlining moves code between symbols, so only whole-program totals compare.
4. **A `-march=native` build and a `-march=x86-64-v3` build of the same
   compiler do not produce the same output**, because the host build's feature
   set reaches the compiler's own target defaults. Byte-identity gates must
   compare like with like; both were run separately here.

## What landed

**The row walks read a published opcode row, not the descriptor
(-91,02 M, -0,950%).** The FAST allocator's prepass asked five questions of
every one of 1,7 M machine rows — the operand roles, the constraint predicate,
the call and terminator attributes, the clobber set, the indirect-branch
identity — and every one of them is a function of the opcode alone. They are
table content. `machine_opcode_rows_prewarm` publishes them as sixteen bytes an
opcode under the serial-initialization contract, so the walk reads one line of
a 6 KB table instead of five fields spread over two lines of an 88-byte
descriptor it touched for nothing else. Measured before: the constraint
predicate alone was **46,33 M** (`machine.c:1441`, inlined into both the
prepass and placement), the per-slot role ladder 39,96 M
(`register_allocator_fast.c:1078-1082`), the loop header 31,15 M, the
indirect-branch pair 21,83 M, the clobber `or` 15,45 M. The roles are now one
shift of a twelve-bit field; the operand kinds are three compares of the four
inline refs whose answers are three nibbles, and the block lanes and virtual
lanes are walked by trailing-zero count, so the slot loop runs once per operand
that has work rather than four times with a branch ladder inside.

**The encoder's capacity pass and its published encodings (-69,51 M,
-0,732%).** Three things it did per row that it does not need to. The capacity
pre-pass was a nine-arm switch over the opcode of every row to add a number
that is a function of that opcode (14,74 M measured, of which 7,40 M is the
switch itself); it is now the same published row's `encode_budget`, with only
switches and aggregate copies — 2% of the stream — taking a predicted-not-taken
arm. Every published GPR encoding record is exactly one sixteen-byte line and
every row was budgeted at least twenty-four bytes, so copying a 1-15 byte
encoding is a whole-line load and store the compiler folds inline; it was a
libc `memcpy` with a length the caller could not see, **2,0 M calls a compile
at ~15 Ir of call overhead each** (1.104.441 from `machine_x64_emit_exact_frame_chunk`
at 15,0 Ir a call, 928.086 from `machine_x64_emit_exact_recipe` at 15,2).
And the disp32 patch spelled one little-endian store as a four-iteration byte
loop, beside a fixup path that has always written it as one store.

**A patched field is one store, not a byte loop (-14,68 M, -0,156%).** The same
shape at the two remaining sites: every patched immediate and displacement went
through a loop over the field's bytes with a trip count the caller cannot fold.
The widths the tables publish are 1, 2, 4 and 8.

**An allocation that must be zero does not re-zero what the OS zeroed
(-56,69 M Ir, which is -57,9 MB of memory written).** A fresh mapping's pages
are zero and an arena hands its bytes out in one direction, so the only part of
a new allocation that can hold anything is the part below the arena's
allocation high-water mark — what a temporal rewind or a pooled reuse left
behind. `arena_allocate_zeroed` clears exactly that overlap. The arena already
carried the mark (`dirty_position`, with `position` supplying its live half so
no store is paid per allocation) and `2026-08-24T131821Z` already did this by
hand for the x86 template cache; this is the general form. The fill census that
ranked its targets, by call site, is the other half of the finding:

The six zero-filled single allocations took the change; together they are the
57,9 MB. Everything left in that table is either a **0xff** fill, which the
pristine path cannot serve (see the ranking below for what would), or a
per-function scratch arena, which is dirty by construction.

**The matching-delimiter query is a windowed scan of the shape sidecar
(v3-neutral, census-priced at ~-58 M with AVX-512).** Both scans — the
lowering's and the parser's — asked the same question one token at a time:
starting at `open` with depth zero, where does the depth return to zero. A
token's shape byte answers it alone, because a punctuator's shape is
`C_TOKEN_SHAPE_PUNCTUATOR | its id`, so the scan can read the one-byte sidecar
instead of reaching into a twelve-byte row for one field, and classify 64
tokens at once: the openers and the closers of a window are two masks, and the
walk then visits only the lanes that can change the depth. The window skip is
what makes a long range cheap — a window can only reach depth zero if it holds
at least `depth` closers, so when it does not, the whole window is two
population counts and an add.

Its census, from an instrumented build over the stage-1 workload: **286.263
queries visiting 13.093.107 tokens** (45,7 a query), of which **1.724.513
(13,2%) are the delimiters being counted**, over **429.386 windows of 64**. The
row scan they replace measures **73,07 M** (`c_gen` 59,60 M, `c_parse`
13,46 M), which is 5,58 Ir a visited token. The windowed form is one masked
load and two compares a window, about five instructions a delimiter lane and
about six of setup a query: **~14,6 M, so ~-58 M**, and the window skip is not
credited in that figure. Lane accounting for the Zen 4 requirement: every lane
of the load is useful (the sidecar is one byte a token, the range contiguous)
and both compares are 64 of 64, so the trade is positive on a double-pumped
256-bit datapath as well.

## Measured negative — do not retry

- **The 64-bit spelling of the delimiter scan: +13,8 M.** Eight tokens a step,
  the two masks out of the classic byte-equality and movemask identities, is
  *slower* than the row scan it replaces, because the ranges are short (45,7
  tokens a query) and the per-query setup plus two windows of mask arithmetic
  outweigh a loop whose branch predicts. The width is the whole of the win.
  Only the 64-lane path is compiled; the row scan stays for everything else.
- **The borrow-based zero-byte marker cannot locate the byte it detects.**
  `(d - 0x0101..) & ~d & 0x8080..` is the textbook `haszero`, and it is exact
  as a *detector*; used as a locator it also marks the byte above a match
  whenever that byte differs from the target by exactly one, because the match
  borrows into it. For shape bytes "differs by one" is the neighbouring
  punctuator id, so `(` immediately followed by `)` reported two openers and
  the compiler mis-parsed its own source. The saturating form,
  `~(((d & 0x7f7f..) + 0x7f7f..) | d | 0x7f7f..)`, cannot carry between lanes
  and is exact; both were checked over all 65.536 adjacent-byte pairs for four
  targets before the second was believed. This cost a build cycle and is the
  only correctness bug this round produced.
- **Zeroing the selector's per-value block through the pristine path.** It is a
  per-function scratch arena that is reset for every one of 4.417 functions, so
  its dirty prefix always covers the whole allocation and the test is pure
  overhead. The 61,5 MB it writes needs the layout change below, not this one.

## Left standing, ranked, priced against the finished tree

Shares are against **9.351,43 M**, where 1% = 93,5 M. Fill figures are megabytes
written, which this instrument reports directly and `instructions:u` does not.

- **The lowering is 40,7% of the compile** and is where the next round belongs.
  Its two halves are `c_ir_lower_body` (2.264 M) and `c_ir_global_initializer`
  (1.292 M). Nothing inside it is one big line; it is a hundred token-range
  walks of 10-40 M each. The shape sidecar kernel that landed here is the
  pattern the rest of them want — the delimiter scan was the one query with a
  single definition to replace, and the others (depth-zero comma, statement
  boundary, group facts) each carry their own extra state.
- **The selector's fourteen per-value side arrays: 61,5 MB of fill a compile**
  (40,2 zero + 21,3 "none"), the largest single item left and the one the
  previous audit named as its largest working set. The "none" half is five or
  six `u32` arrays initialized to `UINT32_MAX`; a one-byte present-bitmap
  beside them would take 21,3 MB to about 1,2 MB, at the cost of a load and a
  test on each read. The zero half is genuinely written.
- **The three 0xff fills of 12,7 MB each** — the position index's matching
  delimiters, the lowering's token entities, its token function declarations —
  are 38 MB that the pristine path would take to zero **if their sentinel were
  0 rather than 0xffffffff**: store `index + 1` and read 0 as "none". That is a
  mechanical change to three arrays and their readers, worth 38 MB.
- **The three large copies of the previous audit are now measurable**:
  `__memcpy_avx_unaligned_erms` is 144,78 M Ir here, i.e. that many bytes,
  after this round took 33,85 M of small-call overhead out of it. The builder's
  materialize (`machine_function_builder_finish`, 35,8 MB over 2.653 calls) and
  the codegen flatten are the top two and both are in-place candidates that
  need an API contract change. They no longer need a quiet machine to defend.
- **`c_parse_infer_file_array_bounds`, 492 M in one call** (5,3%), of which
  ~196 M is 431.628 initializer designators at 254 Ir each and 211 M is the
  string-literal count kernel. The bound inference walks every initializer that
  the lowering then walks again to emit its bytes; the two walks are the same
  traversal with different outputs.
- **The encoder's remaining per-row work.** The capacity pass is now a table
  read, but it is still a whole traversal of 1,7 M rows for a number the
  builder could accumulate as it appends them (an additive field on
  `MachineFunction`, ~14 M). The previous audit ranked this and it is still
  right; it wants the debug-gated reference walk beside it.
