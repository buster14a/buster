`2026-08-21d` (Linux x86_64, Zen 4 7940HS; **compare identifier tokens by
interned id, not by spelling**).  Hotspot #4 of the 2026-08-21 stage-1
branch-miss survey.  `string_equal`'s length compare at `string.c:228` was the
single largest source line in that profile — 1.12% of all 25.17 M misses on
its own, 2.59% summed over every inline copy — and its biggest callers were
passes that re-compare identifier *spellings* against fixed keywords once per
token, each one materializing the spelling through `c_token_length`'s
oversized-length guard (another 1.03% of all misses) to answer a question the
token already carries the answer to.

`CToken.symbol` has held the preprocessor's interned identifier id since
proposal 2 (audit `2026-08-08k`).  The ids of the names a pass compares
against are now compile-time constants: `CSymbolWellKnown` in `c_internal.h`
lists them, `c_symbol_table_create` interns that list *first* and in order, so
an enumerator **is** the id its spelling was assigned, and the create-time
`BUSTER_CHECK`s hold the two together (every enumerator has a spelling, every
spelling lands on its own id).  `c_token_is_well_known` is then one immediate
compare; `c_token_in_well_known_set` takes a `u64` of
`C_SYMBOL_WELL_KNOWN_BIT`s and answers any-of in one shift and mask, which is
what the ladders of eleven and fifteen spelling compares in the statement
walker collapse to.  Both keep the spelling fallback for `symbol == 0` —
pasted, synthesized and test-built tokens never passed the intern pass — and
both read the one spelling table for it, so the fast path and the fallback
cannot drift apart.  Converted: `c_ir_named_label_at`, the two per-token
scans in `c_analyze_semantics` (`for` and `__overloadable__`), and all 47
spelling compares inside `c_ir_lower_body_advance`, the statement walker that
held 3.38% of stage-1 misses while mispredicting at 1.9x its cycles share.

Measured on the idle-ish Zen 4 7940HS, CPU-pinned, clang-built Release `ide`,
compiling the unity `ide.c`, nine alternating same-source pairs against the
pre-change binary:

- instructions `10.583.65 G -> 10.537.51 G`, **-46.1 M (-0.436%)**, reproducible
  to ±0.2 M.
- branches `1.964.554 G -> 1.960.494 G`, **-4.06 M (-0.207%)**.
- branch misses fall in eight of the nine pairs, by roughly `0.2-0.6 M`
  (`1-2%`).  The counter is the noisy one here: co-tenant builds on the same
  box moved the *baseline* between `25.5 M` and `28.7 M` across the session,
  a spread several times the effect, so treat the instruction and branch
  numbers as the result and the miss delta as directionally confirmed only.
  That is the expected shape: the mispredicting branch is the *identity*
  question, which the conversion makes cheaper rather than removing — what
  disappears is the length compare, the emptiness test, the pointer-identity
  test and the `memory_compare` call around it.

Adding names to the well-known list also widens the interned predefined range,
so `__overloadable__` and `asm` now get their class bits from
`c_parse_token_class_compute` at table-create time instead of falling to the
spelling path; both compute zero extra bits, so the two classification paths
still agree.  Gate: `test_self_host` reaches its byte-identical fixed point
(`SELF_HOST deterministic bytes=35384984`), `test_all_combinations_ci` green
locally (Linux only — Windows and aarch64 remain remote-pending).

Left untaken: the `c_analyze_semantics` C23-keyword scan just above the
`__overloadable__` loop compares five spellings per token of an unnamed
declaration, but it is gated on the C23 dialect and on `!declaration->name.length`
and never showed in the profile.  The `switch` statement walker is still an
if/else chain of single-name tests; the next step there is a dense
`symbol -> keyword` byte table and a real `switch`, which is a restructure
rather than a substitution and wants its own measurement.
