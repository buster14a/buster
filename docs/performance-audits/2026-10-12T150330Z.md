## The 77-lookup miss

The first candidate gated eleven statement keywords. Its probe still made
11,853 scope lookups, 77 over the declared ceiling of 11,776.

**What the census had counted.** The ceiling was computed from a census that
counted keywords with the wider `C_IR_STATEMENT_KEYWORD_SET`. That set
includes `STATIC_ASSERT` and the three asm words. The first candidate left
all four out, on the stated ground that some dialect spells them as ordinary
identifiers.

**What the 77 are.** A throwaway census build of the base, not committed,
printed each of the 77 unbound first tokens:

| Spelling | Symbol | Count |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `_Static_assert` | `C_SYMBOL_WELL_KNOWN_STATIC_ASSERT` | 75 |
| `__asm__` | `C_SYMBOL_WELL_KNOWN_ASM_GNU_ALT` | 2 |

None is plain `asm` or `static_assert`.

**What the frontend's source says about each word:**
- **`STATIC_ASSERT` is the reserved `_Static_assert`**
  (`c_symbol_well_known_spellings`, `c_source.c`). The preprocessor respells
  C23's `static_assert` to `_Static_assert`. An older dialect's
  `static_assert` keeps its own symbol and is never gated.
- **`ASM_GNU` and `ASM_GNU_ALT` are the reserved `__asm` and `__asm__`.**
  The parser always reads them as asm keywords (`C_PARSE_ASM_KEYWORDS`).
- **Plain `asm` (`ASM`) is dialect-dependent.** The tree's word table admits
  it as a keyword in the GNU dialects only (`C_AST_W("asm", ASM, GNU)`).
  Elsewhere it is an ordinary identifier and may name a typedef.

So the first candidate's ground held for plain `asm` alone. The miss is
recorded as a miss, and the first audit stands as published.
