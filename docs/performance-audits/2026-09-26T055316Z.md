`2026-09-26T055316Z` (Linux x86-64, 4-vCPU Xeon container (callgrind cache
simulation); no timing host; **validation-pass visiting and function-at-a-time

## Decision

Two individually modest changes to canonical IR preparation remove each
other's limiting factor in the one resource they share: the IR rows of a module
that does not fit in the last-level cache. The change is implemented in PR
#1406 in three reviewable commits:

- **A**, `343ed8d`: validation is visited one function at a time.
- **B**, `207ea44`: preparation runs one function at a time.
- **B′**, `8e0ee56`: an adverse-case fix to B.

Output is byte-identical everywhere it was checked, and no validation is
removed or weakened.

The interaction is established **in a deterministic mechanism metric only**:
simulated last-level-cache data read misses, from callgrind 3.22 with a 32 MiB
16-way LL. On the frozen stage-1 self-compile in the trusted checked
configuration:

- A alone saves 3.23 M preparation read misses.
- B alone saves none (it is a no-op there, as predicted).
- A+B saves 9.51 M, which is 63.1% of preparation's misses and 26.5% of the
  whole compile's.

The interaction term is I = −6.30 M. It is super-additive by about three cold
module sweeps, which is what the frozen plan predicted. The same holds with
`-g0` (I = −6.38 M) and with `-fno-frontend-ssa` (I = −6.89 M). In the
unchecked production configuration it is smaller: I = −1.86 M, the one sweep
predicted. On a held-out real TU, `c_gen.c`, A alone is slightly worse, B alone
does nothing, and A+B is 19% better (I = −208 K).

**No wall-time, cycle or artifact-latency effect is claimed.** The approved
exclusively leased 9700X route is disabled; its admission and deployment (#880)
were out of scope. Simulated misses model neither prefetching nor
memory-level parallelism, so this audit cannot say whether the eliminated
traffic is a substantial end-to-end improvement. Treat it as a mechanism
result with a ready experiment, not a new performance regime.

## Hypothesis and why it is new

Between lowering and codegen, `ir_prepare_canonical_module` ran whole-module
sweeps. The unity self-compile's rows and values are several times a 32 MiB
LLC, so every sweep re-streamed the module from memory. In the trusted Release
build (tests embedded, so `BUSTER_IR_TRANSFORM_CHECKS` is 1) the sweeps are:

1. promotion (P);
2. its output validation (V);
3. FAST (F);
4. FAST's output validation (V);
5. CFG publication (Pub).

Inside `ir_validate_canonical_module`, the ownership walk of every function ran
before the checks of any function. Each V is therefore two cold sweeps, which
makes seven in all.

#791 and #792 priced scan removal and a FAST→publish→select reorder. They
treated the validator as an immovable module-wide barrier, and #564 judged the
validator's *instruction* cost proportionate. The new causal element is
locality:

- The barrier semantics can be kept exactly while the validator's per-function
  work moves into the loop that just touched the rows. The all-or-none FAST
  guard is decided after the loop, and first-error precedence is by category,
  not by visit order.
- The validator's real-hardware cost is dominated by cold misses. On the 9700X
  it was 5.7% of cycles and 15.9% of DRAM fills, against 2.9% of Ir.

Neither part suffices alone:

- **A** fuses the ownership walk with the checks, but each validation still
  runs in its own cold module-wide loop.
- **B** cannot fuse across a whole-module validation. In the checked build one
  sits between every pair of sweeps, so B alone changes nothing there. In the
  unchecked build only F+Pub fuse.

The plan was frozen at 2026-09-26T04:10:44Z, before any variant measurement.
Its SHA-256 is `ac65911b7d1521b0f0593365d17d677900716bde7157cf6cc3926bfda4016eb6`.
It predicted these cold preparation sweeps:

| Configuration | base | A | B | A+B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| checked | 7 | 5 | 7 | 2 |
| unchecked | 5 | 4 | 4 | 2 |

It also fixed the metric, the controls and five refutation criteria.
Disclosure: a disposable A+B screening prototype was measured once before the
plan was written, and it motivated the plan. It is excluded from all tables
below.

## Both sides of the thresholds

**Last-level cache, 256 MiB ("fits").** The checked stage-1 preparation read
misses are 1,691,553 / 1,737,027 / 1,705,612 / 1,861,079 (base / A / B / A+B).
The large saving disappears, which supports the LLC threshold (R3).

A+B also carries a small **cost** here: +169.5 K preparation misses and
+406.5 K (+8.0%) whole-run. The whole compile touches about 1.1 GB, so even
256 MiB does not hold everything. The base's back-to-back module sweeps reuse
rows that lowering left resident, whereas the fused loops interleave more data
per function. The extra misses land in promotion (+145 K), FAST (+111 K) and
selection (+180 K).

**Private cache, 1 MiB model.** A single 8,192-statement `large_function`
exceeds the modelled cache. Its preparation misses are 1.406 M in base and
1.412 M in every variant, so there is no benefit when one function's own
working set does not fit. The 1,024-statement version fits and is unchanged
(12.4 K).

At 32,768 statements the function fails identically in every variant with
`validation failed at arena.h:191`, the pre-existing #1330 lowering-scratch
limit.

**Held-out real TUs.** These are non-unity compiler sources compiled
standalone from the frozen tree with `-g -c`.

| TU (32 MiB LL) | Result |

## Small and adverse populations

- **Synthetic harness corpus** (ci and smoke profiles, 16 files: tiny startup,
  many functions, large function, symbol table, control flow, backend pressure,
  macros, aggregate ABI). All modules fit, so preparation misses are ≤ 16 in
  every variant.
  - Per-call overhead of the pass is small: `tiny_startup` +1.6 K Ir, and
    `many_functions` (512 functions) +22 K preparation Ir (+0.4%, +0.01%
    whole-run).
  - `backend_pressure` (ci) gains +9.7 K whole-run misses (+6% of a small
    total). They are all in `machine_select_canonical_function_x86_64`, which
    reads rows in a different recency state after fused preparation.
- **No-change module.** 2,048 functions that FAST cannot change; this is the
  one case where fusion adds work.
  - In the checked build, A+B visited the FAST output check on every function
    although the base skips it when nothing changed: +4.49 M Ir, +1.16% of the
    compile. B′ starts the check at the first changed or reopened function and
    reduces this to +131 K (+0.034%).
  - The unchecked build was never affected: +135 K Ir (+0.035%).

## Charges

- **Code size (`.text`)**, relative to base:
  - checked native: A +2,672 B, B +0 (its fused branch is compile-time dead),
    A+B +10,672 B, A+B′ +13,744 B;
  - unchecked native: A +1,344 B, B +192 B, A+B +2,064 B.
- **Buffers.** The ownership scratch array is now allocated and rewound per
  function instead of once. Its high-water mark is the same, set by the
  largest function, and it is released before that function's checks.
  `IrValidationPass` lives on the stack. No retained allocation is added, but
  retained FAST pools and published CFG arrays now interleave per function in
  the program arena. Addresses change; bytes emitted do not.
- **Fallback frequency.** The promotion loop visits only when a post-promotion
  validation is certain, that is, FAST runs on unproven rows. That is the
  default driver path. `-fno-canonical-fast`, disabled promotion and
  uncertified input keep the module-wide call, now internally fused by A.
- **Cleanup.** There is no new lifetime or ownership.

## Timing: not measured, and the experiment that would decide it

No local or hosted wall time is evidence. The container's L3 (260 MiB) is on
the "fits" side of the threshold, and its page-fault cost varies with guest
memory history: the same compile took 0.5 s or 2.9 s of system time.

On an approved exclusively leased 9700X (32 MiB L3), use the frozen-source
self-host workload of the existing harness. Measure base versus A, B and A+B′,
built serially in one root, with the same-root rebuild and cross-root controls
of `tools/throughput/DEDICATED.md`. Estimate
I = d(A+B′) − d(A) − d(B) on paired log-ratios using the harness's block
intervals.

- **Predicted:** d(B) ≈ 0 in checked builds, and I < 0 with |I| a sizeable
  fraction of preparation's share of the compile (11.45% of sampled cycles on
  the 9700X, #906).
- **Refuting results:** I inside the A/A band; d(A+B′) not below d(A); or no
  reduction in `ls_any_fills_from_sys.dram_io_all` within preparation.

A useful companion, which needs no timing, is the same four-variant
comparison on a 32 MiB-L3 host with `tools/cache_miss_survey.py` DRAM-fill
attribution.

## Limits

- Callgrind's two-level model has no prefetcher, no memory-level parallelism
  and no L2. The 9700X survey of an older revision measured 13.0 M stage-1
  DRAM fills (2026-09-17T191324Z), against 35.8 M simulated LL read misses
  here, so absolute savings on hardware will be smaller.
- One host, one toolchain (Clang 18 host builds) and an `x86-64-v3` simulation
  build.
- The unity stage-1 input is the compiler itself. External real sources were
  checked for correctness only (cJSON), because Lua and SQLite are blocked
  upstream of preparation on main.
- Adverse costs exist on the "fits" side and must be weighed against any
  measured gain: +8% simulated misses at 256 MiB, up to +2.5% on small real
  TUs, and +6% on one synthetic module.
- A batch runner was stopped once mid-job to add a job lock. The partial job
  was discarded and rerun, and no completed record was replaced.
